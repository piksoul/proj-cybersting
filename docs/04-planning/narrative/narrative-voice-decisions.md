# Narrative Voice Decisions

## Purpose

Deciding who tells the story, from what perspective, and at what distance. Fundamental choices that shape reader experience.

Reference: `docs/craft/narrative-voice.md`

---

## Key Questions

1. **Who will TELL the story?** (Narrator)
2. **Who will SEE the story?** (Focaliser)
3. **What tense?** (Past/Present)
4. **How close?** (Distance)
5. **Reliable or unreliable?**

---

## Narrator Type Options

### By Involvement

| Type | Definition | Fit for Our Story |
|------|------------|-------------------|
| **Heterodiegetic** | Narrator NOT a character | Allows distance; genre-typical |
| **Homodiegetic** | Narrator IS a character | Would need to be Elias or observer |
| **Autodiegetic** | Narrator IS protagonist | Elias tells his own story |

## Point of View Options

### Option A: First Person (Elias)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Voice** | Elias narrates his own experience |
| **Access** | Only what Elias knows, sees, thinks |
| **Advantage** | Maximum intimacy; reader bonded to protagonist |
| **Limitation** | Can't show faction scenes without Elias; limited scope |
| **Genre Fit** | Literary thriller (le Carré's later work); less common in techno-thriller |
| **Risk** | If Elias is too closed-off, voice becomes cold |

**Example tone:**
> "The message came at 3 AM, which meant nothing good. Messages at 3 AM never meant anything good. I stared at the screen, watching the cursor blink, and felt twelve years of careful distance collapse into nothing."

### Option B: Third Person Limited (Elias)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Voice** | Third person narrator, filtered through Elias's perception |
| **Access** | Elias's thoughts and perceptions; no other minds |
| **Advantage** | Intimacy + slight distance; can describe Elias externally |
| **Limitation** | Still restricted to Elias's scenes |
| **Genre Fit** | Most common for thrillers; Gibson uses this |
| **Risk** | Can feel generic if voice isn't distinctive |

**Example tone:**
> "The message came at 3 AM. Elias stared at the screen, watching the cursor blink, and felt twelve years of careful distance collapse into nothing. He'd built walls, protocols, routines. None of them mattered now."

### Option C: Third Person Multiple POV

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Voice** | Third person; shifts between characters by chapter/section |
| **Access** | Multiple characters' perceptions |
| **Advantage** | Scope; can show faction activity; dramatic irony |
| **Limitation** | Fragments reader attachment; more characters to voice |
| **Genre Fit** | Clancy-style techno-thriller; epic scope |
| **Risk** | Dilutes Elias's centrality; can feel sprawling |

**Potential POV characters:**
- Elias (primary ~60%)
- ARCHITECT (villain perspective ~15%)
- Martinez (faction perspective ~10%)
- Shaw (ally/enemy perspective ~10%)
- Father (emotional stakes ~5%)

### Option D: Third Person Omniscient

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Voice** | God-like narrator; knows all, can enter any mind |
| **Access** | Unlimited |
| **Advantage** | Maximum flexibility; can comment on theme |
| **Limitation** | Feels dated; harder to create intimacy |
| **Genre Fit** | Classic spy fiction (early le Carré); less common now |
| **Risk** | Distance from protagonist; preachy if not careful |

## Working Hypothesis

### Recommended: Third Person Limited (Elias) with Possible Expansion

**Primary approach**: Third person limited, Elias's POV throughout

**Rationale:**
1. Maintains intimacy with protagonist
2. Genre-appropriate (Gibson, modern le Carré)
3. Allows external description of Elias
4. Creates mystery (reader discovers with Elias)
5. Emergence can remain mysterious (we only see what Elias perceives)

**Optional expansion**:
- 1-2 ARCHITECT chapters (villain perspective; raises stakes)
- These would be clearly demarcated (different chapter heading, perhaps different tense)

### Why Not First Person?

- Elias's closed-off nature might make first person voice too cold
- Backstory reveals are smoother in third person
- Technical thriller elements easier to convey with slight distance
- Emergence communion at climax may need external perspective

### Why Not Full Multiple POV?

- Dilutes the personal journey
- Our character count is already calibrated for focused story
- le Carré's precision over Clancy's sprawl
- Found family and father work better as external mysteries

## Focalisation

### Who We Follow

| Focaliser | % of Story | Function |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Elias** | 85-95% | Protagonist; primary experience |
| **ARCHITECT** | 5-15% (optional) | Antagonist perspective; dramatic irony |

### Focalisation Rules

1. **No head-hopping mid-scene** - POV shifts only at chapter/section breaks
2. **Clear signals** - Different chapter headings for non-Elias POV
3. **Elias dominates** - His POV is the default; others are exceptions
4. **Mystery preserved** - We learn about ARCHITECT through Elias's discovery, with only occasional direct access

## Distance

### Distance Spectrum

| Level | Example | When to Use |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Remote** | "The city sprawled beneath grey winter clouds." | Scene-setting; transitions |
| **Near** | "Elias moved through the crowd, scanning faces." | Action sequences |
| **Close** | "His chest tightened. Not now. Not here." | Emotional moments |
| **Intimate** | "The fear was old, familiar, a tenant he couldn't evict." | Internal revelation |

### Distance Strategy

| Story Phase | Recommended Distance | Rationale |
|-------------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Opening** | Close to Intimate | Bond reader to Elias immediately |
| **Action sequences** | Near | Pace and clarity |
| **Backstory reveals** | Intimate | Emotional weight |
| **Faction confrontations** | Near to Close | Tension + interiority |
| **Found family scenes** | Close | Warmth and stakes |
| **Climax** | Variable (pull back for scope, zoom in for emotion) | Maximum impact |
| **Resolution** | Intimate to Near | Earned peace |

## Tense Options

### Past Tense (Recommended)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Feel** | Story being recounted; traditional |
| **Advantage** | Most natural for thriller; allows reflection |
| **Example** | "The message came at 3 AM." |

### Present Tense

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Feel** | Immediate; reader in the moment |
| **Advantage** | Urgency; literary contemporary feel |
| **Risk** | Can feel exhausting over novel length; limits reflection |
| **Example** | "The message comes at 3 AM." |

### Recommendation: Past Tense

Past tense is:
- Genre-conventional for thriller
- Allows backstory integration more naturally
- Enables reflection without breaking narrative
- Less fatiguing over 85k words

## Voice Characteristics

### Elias's Perceived World

How should the prose feel when filtered through Elias?

| Quality | Expression in Prose |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Technical precision** | Clean, efficient sentences; specific details |
| **Controlled emotion** | Understatement; emotion shown through action |
| **Hacker's eye** | Sees systems, patterns, vulnerabilities |
| **Climber's awareness** | Physical precision; spatial awareness |
| **Guarded interiority** | Thoughts revealed reluctantly; walls visible |

### Voice Sample (Working)

> The message came at 3 AM, encrypted with a key he'd written twelve years ago and never expected to see again. Elias sat in the blue glow of his monitors, the city silent below, and watched the cursor blink. Three seconds to decrypt. Another two to read. Less than one to understand that everything he'd built since then—the distance, the silence, the careful ordinary life—meant nothing now.
>
> He thought about not responding. The climbing wall in the morning, the coffee with Leon, the easy rhythm of days that belonged only to him. He could delete the message, burn the key, pretend he'd never seen it.
>
> But he'd learned something about pretending, back when pretending was all he had. It never held. The past didn't stay buried just because you stopped looking at it.
>
> He began to type.

## Unreliable Narrator Considerations

### Is Elias Reliable?

| Aspect | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Factual reliability** | Yes - what he observes is accurate |
| **Emotional reliability** | Partially - he minimises his own feelings |
| **Self-knowledge** | Limited - doesn't fully understand his own motivations |
| **Omissions** | Yes - he doesn't tell reader everything immediately |

### Implication

Elias is **reliable about events** but **unreliable about himself**. This allows:
- Backstory to surface gradually
- Emotional revelations to land
- Self-deception as character flaw
- No twist where "it was all a lie"
