## Background

### Origin

Born in Berlin in 1993, three years after reunification. Grew up in a city still finding itself—East and West bleeding together, old systems collapsing, new ones emerging. His parents were East Germans who adapted quickly; they taught him that connections matter more than ideology.

### Education of a Fixer

Dominik learned early:
- Everyone needs something
- Most things can be found if you know who to ask
- Discretion is worth more than the transaction
- Never be the only one holding a secret

By his early twenties, he was the person people called when they needed things—club entry, party supplies, documents, introductions. Nothing violent, nothing ideological. Just the grease that keeps underground Berlin moving.

### The Movement Connection

Dominik came to breaking through the club scene—same circles, overlapping communities. He's not competition-level, but he trains enough to belong. The movement scene is his social life; the fixing is his business. He keeps them separate except when someone needs something.

### Worldview

Dominik believes in:
- **Mutual benefit**: Every deal should work for both sides
- **Information as currency**: What you know is worth more than what you have
- **Staying small**: Big fish get caught; he stays in the middle
- **No ideology**: Left, right, legal, illegal—just transactions
- **Exit strategy**: Always know how to walk away

### Flaws

- **Mercenary**: Would sell information for the right price
- **Slippery**: Hard to pin down, harder to trust completely
- **Avoidant**: Disappears when things get too hot
- **Shallow**: Lots of contacts, few real connections
- **Overconfident**: Thinks he can handle anything; hasn't been truly tested

## Relationship to Elias

### How They Met

Through the movement scene, two years ago. Dominik noticed the American who trained seriously but stayed quiet. Recognized something—not a cop, not a user, just a guy with a past he wasn't discussing. Dominik respects that. He has his own things he doesn't discuss.

### Current Dynamic

They're friendly but not friends. Dominik knows Elias could be a useful contact—clearly has skills, clearly has history, clearly has money from somewhere. Elias knows Dominik could be useful if things go sideways. Neither has tested the relationship.

- **Dominik's view**: Elias is a potential client who hasn't bought anything yet
- **Elias's view**: Dominik is a resource to be used carefully
- **The boundary**: Neither has asked the other for anything real. Yet.

### Trust Level

Dominik wouldn't betray Elias casually—bad for reputation, and he genuinely likes him. But if serious pressure came (legal, financial, physical), Dominik would prioritize himself. Elias probably knows this. It's why he hasn't asked for anything important.

## Story Function

### Thematic Role

Dominik represents:
- **Berlin underground** (the gray market that makes the city work)
- **Transactional relationships** (contrast to found family)
- **Moral ambiguity** (not villain, not hero—just practical)
- **The cost of help** (nothing is free)
- **Unreliable ally** (useful but not trustworthy)

### Plot Functions

| Function | How |
|----------|-----|
| **Resource** | Provides something Elias can't get through official channels |
| **Betrayal potential** | Could sell information to factions if pressured |
| **Underground connection** | Door to Berlin's gray world |
| **Complication** | His help comes with strings |
| **Exposition** | Can explain how Berlin's underground works |

### Chekhov's Gun

If Dominik is introduced as a fixer who could help, **he must be used OR he must betray**. The gun has two barrels:
1. Elias needs him and he delivers (resource fires)
2. Elias needs him and he sells him out (betrayal fires)

A third option: Elias specifically avoids using him because he doesn't trust him, and that choice has consequences.

## Notes for Development

### Questions to Resolve

- Does Dominik help or betray? (Could do both—helps first, betrays under pressure later)
- What's the price of his help? (Money? Favor owed? Information?)
- Does he have any real loyalty, or is it all transaction?
- Does he appear again after his main function, or is he one-and-done?

### Balancing the Character

**Risk**: Dominik as pure betrayer makes him one-dimensional.
**Risk**: Dominik as reliable helper undermines his established nature.

**Solution**: Make it complicated.
- He helps because he wants to
- He betrays because he has to
- He feels bad about the betrayal
- The betrayal has consequences for him too

### Potential Expansion

If Dominik needs more development:
- His own subplot with whoever pressures him
- Genuine friendship with someone in the crew that complicates betrayal
- A redemption beat where he warns Elias about something despite risk
- His background becomes relevant (parents' East German past, etc.)

### Keep in Mind

Dominik is a **morally gray character in a morally gray role**. He's not evil—he's practical. He's not loyal—he's transactional. He's not a villain—he's a survivor who prioritizes himself.

The interesting question is: **What would it take to make him risk himself for someone else?** That's either his character growth or the thing that proves he can't grow.
