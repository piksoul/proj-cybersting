## Background

### Origin

Born in Novi Sad, Serbia's second city, on the Danube. Working-class family. Father works in a factory; mother is a nurse. One older brother who went into the military. Katya was always the restless one—too much energy, too many questions, too little patience for the expected path.

### Coming to Berlin

Arrived at 19, four years ago. Told her parents she was going to study. Actually came for the breaking scene and to escape the weight of expectations. Started with nothing, worked service jobs, trained obsessively. Now she's good—competition good. Maybe great.

### Family Back Home

- Sends money when she can (guilt and love mixed)
- Talks to her mother weekly; father less often
- Hasn't been home in two years
- Brother doesn't approve of her "dancing"—they don't talk
- Carries the weight of being the one who left

### The Breaking Scene

Katya found breaking as a teenager in Novi Sad—small scene, same faces, but it was hers. In Berlin, she discovered a world. Competition. Progression. People who took it seriously. She wants to go professional—battles, sponsorships, the Olympic pathway. It's not a hobby. It's her life.

### Worldview

Katya believes in:
- **Earning everything**: Nothing is given; everything is taken
- **Action over talk**: Words are cheap; show her what you can do
- **Loyalty**: Her people are her people; everyone else is competition
- **The scene**: Breaking is a family, a culture, a way of life
- **Forward motion**: The past is gone; only the next move matters

### Flaws

- **Aggressive**: Pushes too hard, damages relationships
- **Impatient**: Wants results now; frustrated by process
- **Deflects**: Uses challenge to avoid vulnerability
- **Naive**: Hasn't learned that some things can't be outworked
- **Pride**: Won't ask for help until it's almost too late

## Relationship to Elias

### How They Met

Katya noticed Elias at outdoor sessions—the American who moved like he'd been trained. Too old for competition but too good for a hobbyist. She challenged him the way she challenges everyone. He didn't get offended. They started training together.

### Current Dynamic

Over two years, competition became respect became something neither has named. She pushes him; he protects her (quietly, in ways she pretends not to notice). They're not friends exactly—more like siblings who never had to negotiate childhood.

- **Katya's role**: Challenges his walls; demands presence; represents the future
- **Elias's role**: Protective without patronizing; sees her potential; won't let her repeat his mistakes
- **Unspoken bond**: Neither acknowledges it directly, but both would show up for the other

### What Katya Sees in Elias

> He's the best mover she's trained with, but something's wrong. He holds back—not from lack of skill, but from something inside. There's a coiled quality to him, like he's ready for a fight that isn't coming. She doesn't know what broke him, but she knows broken when she sees it. She pushes him because someone should. Because maybe he needs someone who won't handle him carefully.

### What Elias Sees in Katya

> She reminds him of himself at that age—before Prometheus, before the compromises. Driven, talented, certain the world would make sense if she just worked hard enough. He can't save his younger self. He couldn't protect Sarah. Maybe he can keep Katya from becoming what he became. Maybe that's enough.

## Story Function

### Thematic Role

Katya represents:
- **Youth and potential** (what Elias is fighting to protect)
- **Directness** (cuts through his walls where others can't)
- **The future** (life goes on; new people matter)
- **Non-romantic female bond** (refreshing in genre)
- **Found family** (what he's built without realizing)

### Plot Functions

| Function | How |
|----------|-----|
| **Stakes** | Her safety can be threatened to pressure Elias |
| **Truth-teller** | Says what others won't; breaks through his defenses |
| **Witness** | Sees him clearly; represents the "normal world" view |
| **Emotional beat** | Departure scene, possible return at end |

### Chekhov's Gun

If Katya is introduced as someone Elias is protective of, **that protection must be tested**—either she's threatened, or she witnesses the cost of his other life, or she's the reason he fights to come back.

## Notes for Development

### Questions to Resolve

- Is she threatened directly, or is the threat implied?
- Does she learn anything about Elias's real life?
- Is she present at the end (resolution), or does the promise hang?
- Does she get a moment of agency, or is she primarily stake?

### Avoiding Pitfalls

**Risk**: "Woman in refrigerator"—she exists only to be threatened/hurt to motivate male protagonist.

**Solution**: Give her agency. If she's threatened, she can:
- Fight back (she's an athlete, not helpless)
- Make her own choices about the danger
- Have a scene from her POV
- Matter to the ending beyond being "saved"

### Potential Expansion

If Katya needs more story presence:
- She could discover something about Elias's past (finds something, overhears something)
- She could make contact with a faction character (not knowing who they are)
- She could choose to help despite the danger
- She could represent the "normal world" cost—what happens when spy life touches civilian life

### Keep in Mind

Katya works best as:
- Emotional stakes that feel earned
- Voice of directness that cuts through
- Proof that Elias has built something worth protecting
- Non-romantic relationship (don't default to romance)

Her youth and directness are strengths. Don't soften her into a victim. Even scared, even threatened, she should be herself—fierce, proud, Katya.
