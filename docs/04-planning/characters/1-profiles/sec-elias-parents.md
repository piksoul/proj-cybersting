# ELIAS'S PARENTS — DAVID & ELENA REED

*Blood family. The normal world. The Christmas promise.*

---

## Overview

The parents aren't the primary B-story emotional weight — that's Aunt Astrid. They're the **contrast**: the normal world Elias left behind. Not estranged, not worried in a heavy way — just... there, living their lives, wishing he'd call more.

**The contrast**:

- Elias: Technology is control
- They can't quite talk anymore, not because they're estranged, but because they're seeing different things

## The Family Dynamic

### Not Broken — Just Distant

| Aspect | What It Looks Like |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Love** | Real and present. They're good parents. |
| **Understanding** | Limited. They don't know his world. |
| **Communication** | Surface level. Cars, weather, "how's work?" |
| **Worry** | Mum worries quietly. Dad deflects with enthusiasm. |
| **Estrangement** | None. Just... gap. Different worlds. |

### The Distance Problem

**The Work/Wound Withdrawal**

> After the kompromat event, Elias withdrew from all platforms. Professional paranoia? Probably. But also shame. Easier not to talk than to lie. Easier not to call than to pretend everything's fine.
>
> He deleted social media. Stopped using regular channels. His parents have his Signal number — but he rarely answers. When he does, he sounds tired, distant, "fine."

### What They See vs What They Know

| What Parents See | What's Actually Happening |
|------------------|--------------------------|
| "Elias is busy with work" | He's hiding |
| "Elias is somewhere we can't reach" | He's made himself unreachable |
| "Elias sounds tired" | He's broken |
| "Elias will come home when work settles" | Work will never settle |

## The Christmas Promise

### The Promise

Not dramatic. Just simple.

> MUM: "Promise me you'll come for Christmas. Promise me, Elias."
>
> ELIAS: "I promise."
>
> He said it because it was easier than explaining why he might not. He meant it, in the moment. Whether he can keep it is another question.

### The Weight

The Christmas promise carries extra weight because:
- It's to Mum specifically (she extracted it)
- It represents normalcy — being present, being a son
- It's a deadline — the story may be racing toward it
- It's about presence, not reconciliation (unlike Astrid)

### Promise Configuration

| Option | To Whom | Weight |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Option 1** | To Mum (Parents) | Simple, warm, about presence |
| **Option 2** | To Astrid (Aunt) | Heavy, about facing shame |
| **Option 3** | Both | Parents = destination; Astrid = reckoning |

**Recommendation**: Option 3 — He promised Mum he'd come home. But home means also seeing Astrid. And he's not sure he can do both.
