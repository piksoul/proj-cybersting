### Our Position: The Sweet Spot

At 17-19 characters, we sit between:
- le Carré's surgical precision (every character morally weighted)
- Crichton's ensemble approach (enough to populate the world)

This count allows:
- Multiple faction representation without bloat
- Found family dynamics with room to breathe
- Antagonist complexity through distributed characters
- Enough tertiary characters for world texture

## Comparison: Character Introduction Pacing

### Reference Patterns

**Clancy (Hunt for Red October):**
- Opens with many characters simultaneously
- Reader must track Soviet and American casts
- Characters introduced by role/rank first

**Gibson (Neuromancer):**
- Case alone in opening
- Molly introduced ~chapter 2
- Characters added gradually
- Many remain mysterious

**Crichton (Jurassic Park):**
- Ensemble introduced in sequence
- Each expert gets an entrance
- Clear role establishment

**le Carré (The Spy Who Came In From the Cold):**
- Very few characters initially
- Slow revelation of network
- Characters earn their introductions

### Recommended Approach for Our Story

Given our hybrid genre positioning:

**Act One (Setup):**
- Open with Elias alone or with one crew member
- Introduce found family naturally (climbing scene)
- Dominik appears when needed (resource function)
- Father established through phone call or memory
- One faction representative (Martinez or surveillance)

**Act Two (Confrontation):**
- Faction characters introduced as encountered
- ARCHITECT revealed gradually
- Shaw as potential ally/defector
- Market operators when relevant

**Act Three (Resolution):**
- No new major characters
- Callbacks to established cast
- Convergence, not expansion

## Quantity Guidelines

### Principles

1. **Every named character must serve plot or theme**
2. **Prefer deepening existing characters over adding new ones**
3. **Tertiary characters can be unnamed if functional**
4. **Faction representation ≠ faction cast (one face per faction)**
5. **Found family is fixed; factions are flexible**

### Red Flags (Too Many Characters)

- Reader confusion about who's who
- Characters introduced but never returning
- Multiple characters serving same function
- Scenes requiring character list reference

### Red Flags (Too Few Characters)

- World feels underpopulated
- Protagonist has no one to interact with
- Factions feel like single individuals
- No expendable characters for stakes
