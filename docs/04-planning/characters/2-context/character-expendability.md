## Death Patterns by Genre

### Spy Fiction (le Carré Pattern)

**Characteristics:**
- Protagonists CAN die - the genre allows tragic endings
- Love interests are extremely high risk
- Betrayal often precedes death
- Deaths carry moral weight and consequence
- Nobody is safe (creates genuine tension)

**Notable Deaths:**
- Leamas dies at the Berlin Wall (protagonist)
- Liz dies with him (love interest)
- Fiedler dies because of the operation (innocent ally)

**Theme:** The system consumes everyone it touches.

### Cyberpunk (Gibson Pattern)

**Characteristics:**
- Protagonists survive but are transformed
- Love interests often die or are permanently lost
- Antagonists frequently die (system ejects them)
- Deaths feel inevitable, systemic rather than personal
- The machine world grinds people up

**Notable Deaths:**
- Linda Lee (love interest, dead before story starts)
- Dixie Flatline (exists only as ROM construct)
- Armitage (personality collapses, ejected from helicopter)
- Riviera (killed by Molly post-climax)

**Theme:** The meat is weak; the machine persists.

### Techno-thriller (Clancy/Crichton Pattern)

**Characteristics:**
- Protagonists almost always survive
- Deaths are often "deserved" (hubris, incompetence, villainy)
- Supporting characters die to prove stakes are real
- Competence is rewarded; arrogance is punished
- Body count establishes threat level

**Notable Deaths:**
- Nedry (greed + incompetence = dinosaur food)
- Hammond (plays God, dies in novel)
- Putin (rigid ideology = eliminated)
- Arnold, Muldoon (system overwhelms even competent)

**Theme:** Respect the system or be destroyed by it.

## Death Timing Patterns

### When Characters Die

| Timing | Function | Examples |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **Opening** | Establish stakes, prove threat | Piedmont townspeople (Andromeda), Linda Lee (backstory) |
| **Midpoint** | Raise stakes, remove safety net | Nedry (Jurassic), Arnold (Jurassic) |
| **Climax** | Consequences of final confrontation | Armitage (Neuromancer), Putin (Red October) |
| **Ending** | Thematic statement | Leamas + Liz (Spy), Hammond (Jurassic novel) |

### Death Function Analysis

| Function | Description | Best Used For |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Stakes Establishment** | Prove the threat is real | Early tertiary deaths |
| **Mentor Sacrifice** | Hero must continue alone | Secondary mentor figure |
| **Betrayer's Fate** | Consequences for treachery | Dominik-type characters |
| **Love Lost** | Emotional devastation | Love interest (careful of fridging) |
| **Antagonist Fall** | Justice/nemesis | Primary villain |
| **Tragic Ending** | Thematic statement | Protagonist (rare, powerful) |
| **Heroic Sacrifice** | Character proves worth | Reformed antagonist, ally |

## Archetypes vs. Developed Characters

### The Reality: Hybrid Approach

All reference works use a mix of archetypes and developed characters, distributed by tier.

| Work | Archetype Characters | Developed Characters | Approach |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|----------|
| **le Carré** | Very few | Almost all | Even minor characters have texture |
| **Gibson** | Many (street samurai, console cowboy) | Core 3-4 only | Archetypes elevated by style |
| **Crichton** | Many (corporate villain, skeptic scientist) | Protagonist + 1-2 | Plot-driven, character-supported |
| **Clancy** | Many (by-the-book officer, defector) | Protagonist + key antagonist | Genre conventions embraced |

### How Masters Elevate Archetypes

**Gibson's Technique:**
- Takes the "street samurai" archetype (Molly)
- Adds specific sensory detail: retractable claws, mirror eyes
- Gives her a hidden wound: *broken heart, lost love*
- The archetype becomes memorable through texture

**Crichton's Technique:**
- Uses "corporate saboteur" archetype (Nedry)
- Gives him a specific, relatable grievance (underpaid, disrespected)
- Shows his competence before his failure
- The archetype dies satisfyingly because we understand his wound

**le Carré's Technique:**
- Refuses archetypes almost entirely
- Every character has contradictions
- Even "the villain" (Mundt) is morally complicated
- Requires more page time per character

**Clancy's Technique:**
- Embraces archetypes openly
- Professional competence as characterisation
- Characters defined by role and expertise
- Efficiency over depth for most cast

## The Cutout Question

### What Are Cutouts?

"Cutouts" are flat, purely functional characters who serve plot mechanics without any characterisation. They exist to:
- Provide information
- Die for stakes
- Fill out crowd scenes
- Perform single functions

### Cutout Usage in Reference Works

| Work | Cutout Examples | Function | Named? |
|------|----------------|----------|--------|
| **Hunt for Red October** | Various bridge officers | Procedural authenticity | Some |
| **Neuromancer** | Zion Rastas | World texture, deus ex machina | Group only |
| **Jurassic Park** | Park workers, kitchen staff | Body count | Rarely |
| **The Spy Who Came In From the Cold** | Almost none | Even Control has depth | N/A |
| **Cryptonomicon** | Various WWII soldiers | Historical texture | Rarely |
| **The Andromeda Strain** | Piedmont residents | Mass death stakes | Named for impact |

### Cutout Guidelines

**When to use cutouts:**
- Background authenticity (security guards, staff)
- Single-scene functions (delivers message, dies)
- Crowd and world texture
- Procedural requirements

**When NOT to use cutouts:**
- Any character with more than one scene
- Characters who affect protagonist emotionally
- Characters who make meaningful choices
- Anyone who needs to be remembered

### Expendability Tiers

**Protected (Should Not Die):**
- Elias (unless choosing le Carré tragic ending)
- Katya (found family core, fridging concerns)
- Father (promise must be kept or consciously broken)

**At Risk (Death Serves Story):**
- Dominik (betrayal consequences)
- Shaw (defector fate)
- Vera (doubt meets consequence)
- One faction character (stakes)

**High Risk (Expected to Die):**
- ARCHITECT (antagonist fate, though may be "absorbed" not killed)
- Tertiary faction operatives
- Any character who betrays

**Cutout Tier (Functional Deaths):**
- Unnamed operatives
- Security personnel
- Crowd casualties

## Recommended Death Structure

### Based on Genre Positioning (le Carré meets Gibson)

**Opening Deaths (Stakes):**
- Tertiary character(s) to establish threat
- Possibly connected to inciting incident
- Not main cast

**Midpoint Death (Raising Stakes):**
- One secondary character
- Most likely: Dominik (if he betrays early) or a faction character
- Shows spy world consumes people

**Climax Deaths:**
- ARCHITECT (fate ambiguous - absorbed by emergence?)
- Possibly Shaw (if heroic sacrifice path)
- Vera (if she turns, consequences either way)

**Ending:**
- NOT Elias (choose Gibson survival over le Carré tragedy)
- Found family survives (Leon, Katya)
- Father survives (Christmas promise kept)
- The survival of normal life is the victory

### The Archetype-Depth Distribution

| Tier | Character Approach | Example |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| **Primary** | Full development, contradictions, arc | Elias: skill + trauma + growth |
| **Secondary Core** | Archetype + one specific wound/texture | Leon: mentor archetype + mystery past + "be here now" philosophy |
| **Secondary Extended** | Archetype + one memorable detail | Martinez: institutional man + family photo on desk |
| **Tertiary** | Clean archetype, one memorable line | Weber: by-the-book + single scene defining moment |
| **Cutouts** | Functional only, unnamed if possible | Security guards, crowd, unnamed operatives |

## Chekhov's Deaths

If a character dies, their death should be set up:

| Character | Setup Required | Payoff |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| **Dominik** | Establish transactional nature early | Betrayal → consequences feel earned |
| **Shaw** | Show what she's running from | Death or sacrifice completes her arc |
| **Vera** | Establish her doubt | Crisis point forces choice → consequence |
| **ARCHITECT** | Build his philosophy | Fate reflects his beliefs about emergence |

### Avoid:

- Random deaths for shock value
- Killing characters we haven't invested in
- Deaths that don't serve theme
- Fridging (killing characters only to motivate protagonist)

## Summary Recommendations

| Aspect | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| **Protagonist** | Survives, transformed (Gibson pattern) |
| **Found family** | Protected - survival is victory |
| **Antagonist** | Falls or is absorbed (ambiguous fate) |
| **Betrayer** | Dies (consequences) |
| **Secondary deaths** | 1-2 characters, earned |
| **Tertiary deaths** | Stakes establishment only |
| **Cutouts** | Unnamed, functional |
| **Archetype approach** | Archetypes + texture for secondary; full development for primary |
| **Overall tone** | Spy fiction stakes + techno-thriller survival |

The goal: Readers should feel genuine fear for characters while trusting that survival is possible and meaningful.
