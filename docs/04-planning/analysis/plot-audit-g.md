**Date**: December 2024
**Source**: Yorke's 10 Questions + Structural Checkpoints (docs/craft/plot-audit.md)

---

## Yorke's 10 Questions

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|----------|--------|
| 1 | **Whose story is this?** | Elias Reed — burned-out pattern analyst, haunted by friend Sarah's death |
| 2 | **What do they need?** (internal) | To trust others again. He tried to save Sarah alone and failed. Needs to accept that he can't do it by himself. |
| 3 | **What is the inciting incident?** | Financial system announcement fails; coins move; Elias recognizes patterns from his post-Sarah research — her death was tangentially connected |
| 4 | **What does the protagonist want?** (external) | To understand what's happening, redeem Sarah's death by stopping those responsible |
| 5 | **What obstacles are in the way?** | ARCHITECT's faction; his own isolation/inability to trust; the scale of the crisis; guilt that paralyzes |
| 6 | **What's at stake?** | Personal: Repeating Sarah's fate with crew; his sanity. Global: Financial reset, justice for Sarah's sacrifice |
| 7 | **Why should we care?** | Elias tried to save someone and failed. He's been punishing himself for years. He deserves a second chance — and so does Sarah's memory. |
| 8 | **What does the protagonist learn?** | You can't do it alone. Trust costs, but isolation costs more. Sarah died because he worked alone; the crew survives because they work together. |
| 9 | **How and why do they learn this?** | Through confession (midpoint — tells crew about Sarah), through crew's loyalty, through the climax sequence where trust is the skill being tested. |
| 10 | **How does the story end?** | Crew coordinates precision sequence; Emergence deletes keys and disappears. Naive dreams lost but guilt redeemed. Leon disappears. Home for Christmas. Katya teaches Leon's class. |

## The Law of Opposites

**Central tension**: Control vs. Adaptation (same as F, different expression)

| Elias's Arc | What It Means |
|-------------|---------------|
| Started with control | Tried to save Sarah alone (control the outcome) |
| Failed through control | She died; his solo effort wasn't enough |
| Isolation = control | Pushing everyone away = controlling risk |
| Trust = adaptation | Accepting help = adapting to reality |
| Wins by letting go | Crew succeeds where he failed alone |

**Secondary tension**: Isolation vs. Connection

## Audit Summary

### Strengths Identified

1. **Crew has real weight** — Their skills are essential at climax, not just backstory
2. **Sarah connection adds personal stakes** — Her death makes the crisis matter emotionally
3. **Confession scene is strong midpoint** — Vulnerability as turning point
4. **Trust as skill** — The climax tests character, not just competence
5. **Earthsea twist is the most distinctive option** — If executed well, elevates to literary territory

### Gaps to Address

1. **Sarah's echo line** — What would she say now? Needs specific line that returns at climax.
2. **ARCHITECT visibility** — Risk of antagonist feeling abstract. Need a face/scene.
3. **Emergence nature** — External or internal (Earthsea)? Decision needed.
4. **Precision sequence specifics** — What exactly does each crew member do? Current options (Distraction/Chain/Handoff) need locking.
5. **Sarah connection specifics** — How exactly did her mission cross Network operations? Currently "tangential" but vague.

### Priority Fixes

1. Write Sarah's echo line — the phrase that haunts Elias and returns at climax
2. Decide Emergence: external equalizer vs internal integration (test write both?)
3. Lock precision sequence option (recommend B: Precision Chain or C: Handoff)
4. Add one ARCHITECT scene where he's directly present (even if not confronting Elias)

### Confidence Level

**7/10** — Strong psychological foundation, crew essential, but needs Sarah line, Emergence decision, and sequence specifics before scene list.

## Comparison Notes

**vs. F**: G is richer psychologically but has more moving parts. The wound-test link is good but not as frictionless as F's "AI returns" structure.

**The fork**:
- Standard G = solid crew thriller
- Earthsea G = most distinctive version, highest risk/reward

**Recommendation**: Decide on Emergence nature first. If external → lock precision sequence. If internal → design the integration moment carefully.
