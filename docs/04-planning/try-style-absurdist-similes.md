# Style Experiment: Absurdist Similes

## The Technique

Absurdist similes create comparisons that are:
- Unexpected but weirdly precise
- Funny without undermining tension
- Memorable and quotable
- Revealing of character perspective

The goal isn't random surrealism — it's **finding the comparison that shouldn't work but does**.

---

## Why This Works for Cypherpunk Noir

The crypto/finance/surveillance world is already absurd:
- Imaginary money worth more than countries
- Algorithms deciding fates
- Anonymous creators worshipped like gods
- Numbers that collapse economies

Absurdist similes acknowledge this absurdity while staying grounded. They say: *yes, this is strange, and we're going to describe it strangely*.

---

## Categories & Examples

### Technical Processes Described Humanly

Describing inhuman systems in uncomfortably human terms:

> The blockchain verified transactions with the thoroughness of a mother-in-law inspecting a dinner table.

> The algorithm sorted humans into categories like a child organizing Halloween candy — by color, then by how much it wanted to consume them.

> The encryption held, stubborn as a toddler who'd decided this was not bathtime.

> The network latency stretched the seconds like taffy at a boardwalk stand nobody visits anymore.

### Human Behavior Described Technically

Inverting the above — people as systems:

> He processed the bad news in stages, like a deprecated API handling requests it was never designed for.

> Her smile loaded in segments, starting at the corners and working toward sincerity, never quite achieving full resolution.

> His loyalty was backwards-compatible — it worked with older versions of the organization but crashed when faced with current management.

### Scale & Proportion

Making the incomprehensible graspable through absurd comparison:

> The sum was large in the way that the ocean is wet — technically accurate and completely useless for understanding.

> The debt was the size of a small country, if the country were made of broken promises stacked very carefully.

> The server farm consumed electricity like a teenager consuming validation — constantly, desperately, without visible effect.

### Institutional Absurdity

Bureaucracy and organizations rendered in their true strangeness:

> The committee reached consensus the way glaciers reach the sea — imperceptibly, destructively, and slightly too late to matter.

> The memo circulated through departments like a rumor at a funeral — everyone saw it, nobody claimed to have started it, and it changed slightly with each telling.

> The reorganization was implemented with the grace of a giraffe on roller skates — technically upright, visibly struggling, and impressive mainly for not having collapsed yet.

### Emotional States

Interior experience made exterior through odd comparison:

> Hope sat in his chest like a bird that had forgotten how to fly but refused to admit it.

> The anxiety was specific, not general — like being afraid of one particular spider rather than spiders as a concept.

> His confidence had the half-life of a sandwich in a meeting room — present at first, then simply gone, with no one willing to admit they'd seen it go.

> She felt the fear you feel when you've sent an email to the wrong person but haven't yet realized which email or which person.

### Physical Spaces

Locations rendered through absurdist lens:

> The office was open-plan in the way that a wound is open — technically a feature, functionally a problem.

> The data center hummed with the contentment of a cat that had swallowed something expensive.

> The lobby was designed to impress visitors who'd never been impressed by anything real.

> The safe room was safe the way a promise is safe — contingent on everyone involved continuing to pretend.

## Character-Specific Simile Registers

Different characters might think in different absurdist registers:

### Elias (Cryptographer)

Mathematical and systemic absurdism:

> The meeting devolved into chaos with the predictability of a pseudorandom number generator — technically chaotic, actually inevitable.

> Trust was distributed unevenly, like prime numbers across integers — mostly absent, occasionally clustered, following patterns only visible in retrospect.

### A Field Operative

Physical and survival-oriented:

> The exit was blocked in the way that advice is blocked by someone who's already made up their mind — not impenetrably, just uselessly.

> He moved through the crowd like an apology through a family gathering — noticed, tolerated, not quite welcome.

### A Finance Type

Transactional and material:

> The deal was structured like a marriage of convenience between people who found each other genuinely inconvenient.

> His portfolio was diversified the way a hypochondriac's medicine cabinet is diversified — against threats both real and imagined, with no clear sense of which was which.

### A Hacker/Cypherpunk

Cultural and referential:

> The firewall had the structural integrity of a foam sword at a Renaissance fair — impressive at a distance, useless upon contact.

> Security through obscurity, like hiding your diary by writing it in a font no one likes.

## Similes for Key Story Elements

### The Failed Keys

> The keys didn't work. It was like putting the correct password into a lock and having the lock shrug.

> The cryptographic failure was impossible the way jumping to the moon is impossible — except someone had just jumped to the moon and was now waving.

### The Satoshi Mystery

> Satoshi's absence was present, like the hole a tooth leaves — you keep touching it with your tongue, feeling the shape of what isn't there.

> The wallet sat on the blockchain like an unexploded bomb that had been ticking for fifteen years without anyone noticing there was supposed to be an explosion.

### Emergence

> The network behaved like a flock of birds that had developed opinions — coordinated, unpredictable, and recently interested in something no individual bird had planned.

> Emergence: when the ants build a cathedral and none of them know what a cathedral is.

### The Spectacle

> Reality was filtered through so many layers of narrative that experiencing it directly felt like a violation of terms of service.

> The news was happening to them the way weather happens to people who've forgotten what weather is — as content, not as experience.

## Rhythm & Placement

### Not Every Line

Absurdist similes work best when **earned by contrast**:

1. Establish straight prose
2. Drop absurdist simile at key moment
3. Return to straight prose

**Example:**

> He reviewed the transaction logs. The timestamps were consistent. The amounts matched. The counterparties were verified. Everything was in order, like a crime scene cleaned by someone who'd watched too many tutorials. [SIMILE LANDS BECAUSE IT BREAKS PATTERN]

### Varied Length

Mix short and long:

**Short:**
> The code was elegant. The implementation was not.

**Medium:**
> The meeting ended the way most meetings end — with everyone agreeing to do what they'd already planned to do.

**Long:**
> The protocol had been designed by people who believed in the fundamental goodness of mathematics and the fundamental badness of banks, which meant it worked beautifully until humans became involved, at which point it worked the way a symphony works when half the orchestra has been replaced by people who think they remember how to play the oboe.

## Absurdist Simile Generators

Prompts for creating new ones:

### Structure 1: "[Technical thing] like [domestic/mundane thing]"
- Encryption like a child's secret language
- Blockchain like a family recipe nobody's allowed to modify

### Structure 2: "[Mundane thing] with the [quality] of [technical thing]"
- A smile with the latency of overseas packet transmission
- Handshake with the warmth of a TLS negotiation

### Structure 3: "[Abstract concept] was [physical impossibility]"
- Trust was architectural — load-bearing in places no one had inspected
- Loyalty was geographic — it existed only in territories he controlled

### Structure 4: "[Comparison] except [subverted expectation]"
- Like watching a chess grandmaster, except the pieces were on fire and the grandmaster was crying
- Like a surgeon's precision, except the surgery was on the surgeon, by the surgeon, during a hurricane

## Pitfalls to Avoid

| Pitfall | Example | Problem |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Trying too hard** | "Like a quantum-entangled platypus in a recursive fever dream" | Randomness isn't absurdism |
| **Breaking tension** | Comedy in a death scene | Know when to go straight |
| **Repetitive structure** | Every simile starts with "like a..." | Vary the construction |
| **Obscure references** | "Like Wittgenstein's poker but with DNS servers" | Absurdism should land without Googling |
| **Forced quirkiness** | Similes for their own sake | Every comparison should reveal something |

## Integration with Sardonic Wit

Absurdist similes and sardonic wit are natural partners:

> The oversight committee had the effectiveness of a chocolate teapot, but at least chocolate teapots didn't require per diems. [ABSURDIST + SARDONIC]

> He'd been promoted for his loyalty, which was the corporate equivalent of being praised for your taste in friends after your friends had all been arrested. [ABSURDIST STRUCTURE + SARDONIC OBSERVATION]

The combination creates a voice that's both observant and strange — someone who sees the absurdity and has developed a vocabulary for it.

## Test Passage

Full paragraph using the technique:

> The trading floor was quiet, which was wrong in the way that silence is wrong in a nursery — indicative of either sleep or catastrophe, and nobody wanted to check. Screens displayed numbers that had been moving for so long that stillness seemed like the numbers had died. Martinez stood at his terminal with the posture of a man who'd just learned that his parachute was actually a backpack full of textbooks. Useful, in theory. Fatally incorrect in practice. The market had gapped down thirty percent in the time it took to make coffee, and the coffee — still too hot to drink — was now worth more than the pension fund he'd been managing. He took a sip anyway. It burned in a way that felt appropriate.
