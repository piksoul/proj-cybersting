## The Scene

The warehouse was cold, industrial, somewhere in the outskirts of Prague. Leon had chosen it—neutral ground, he'd said, as if any ground was neutral when you were asking strangers to commit a felony together.

Elias arrived first. Of course he did. The pattern-sight was screaming at him to scope the exits, check the sight lines, map every possible way this could go wrong. He found four exits, two skylights, and a structural weakness in the east wall that could be turned into a fifth if someone brought the right tools.

Leon arrived second, which meant Leon had already been there, watching to see if Elias would show.

"You came," Leon said.

"You knew I would."

"I hoped." Leon settled onto a crate, looking like a man who'd sat on a thousand crates in a thousand warehouses. "The others will be here soon."

"Tell me about them."

"You'll meet them in five minutes."

"Tell me anyway."

Leon smiled—the thin, knowing smile of someone who recognized a compulsion because he shared it. "Katya first. She's your tech. Best I've ever worked with, and I've worked with people who could crack the Pentagon before breakfast. She's quiet, precise, doesn't make mistakes. If she says a system is vulnerable, it's vulnerable."

"And her weakness?"

"She thinks in systems. People are harder for her. She can tell you exactly how a security protocol works; she can't always tell you what the guard is going to do when he gets bored."

"That's why you need me."

"That's part of why I need you."

The warehouse door scraped open. A woman walked in—early thirties, sharp features, the kind of posture that said military or dance, maybe both. She scanned the room the same way Elias had, cataloging exits and threats.

"Katya," Leon said. "This is Elias."

She nodded. Didn't extend a hand. "You're the pattern-reader."

"That's what they call it."

"Does it work?"

"I'm still alive."

Something flickered in her expression—not quite approval, but recognition. "Good enough."

She took a position against the far wall, arms crossed. Not hostile. Just watching.

"And Dominik?" Elias asked.

As if on cue, the door opened again. The man who walked in was built like someone had decided to make a tank out of a person and then given it surprisingly kind eyes. Six-four, easy, with the shoulders of a weightlifter and the hands of someone who'd used them for things that didn't involve lifting weights.

"You started without me," he said. His accent was Eastern European—Polish, maybe—with a warmth that didn't match his frame.

"We were just introducing ourselves," Leon said. "Dominik, this is Elias. Elias, meet the reason we're going to walk out of this job alive."

Dominik extended a hand—massive, calloused, gentle when he took Elias's grip. "Leon talks too much. I just make sure nobody shoots us."

"Security?"

"Protection. There's a difference."

"What's the difference?"

"Security is about systems. Locks, cameras, protocols. Protection is about people. Making sure they don't get hurt." Dominik's eyes were steady. "I protect people. That's what I do."

Elias filed it away. The man was simple—not stupid, but uncomplicated. He believed what he said. In a business full of liars, that was worth something.

"So," Leon said, clapping his hands once. "We're all here. Let me tell you why."

He pulled a tablet from his bag, projected something onto the warehouse wall. Numbers. Account structures. Flow charts showing money moving through shells within shells.

"ARCHITECT," Leon said. "That's what they call him. Nobody knows his real name, his face, his location. What we know is what he does: he launders money for the worst people on the planet. Traffickers, arms dealers, governments that shouldn't have the weapons they're buying. He's invisible, untouchable, and he's gotten rich making other people's crimes disappear."

"We're going to steal from him?" Katya asked. Not skeptical. Clarifying.

"We're going to steal from the people he works for. The money flows through his systems, but it belongs to his clients. We hit the pipeline, redirect to accounts that go back to the victims, and disappear before anyone knows what happened."

"Robin Hood," Dominik said. He was smiling.

"More or less."

"Why us?" Elias asked.

Leon turned to him. "Because you can see the patterns. The timing windows, the security gaps, the path through the maze. Katya can execute once you've mapped it. Dominik makes sure nobody dies. And I—"

"You coordinate."

"I've been running crews since before you were old enough to run from the cops. I know how to put the right people in the right places and get them out again."

Elias looked at the projection. The numbers swam, rearranged themselves into shapes and flows. He could see it—vaguely, incompletely, but the pattern was there. A way through.

"What's our cut?" Katya asked.

"Nothing."

Silence.

"This isn't a profit job," Leon said. "The money goes back. All of it. We walk away clean—no money, no trail, no ties to what we did. The only thing we get is the satisfaction of watching ARCHITECT's clients scream."

"And you think that's enough?" Katya's voice was flat.

"I think you're here because you need something money can't buy. All of you." Leon looked at each of them in turn. "Redemption. Purpose. Proof that you're more than what you've done."

Nobody spoke. The warehouse hummed with silence.

"I'm in," Dominik said. Simply, like he was agreeing to help move furniture.

Katya was quiet longer. Then: "The systems. I want full access to map them before we commit."

"Done."

She nodded. Not enthusiasm. Just agreement.

Leon turned to Elias. "And you?"

Elias looked at them—Leon with his calm authority, Katya with her precision, Dominik with his steady certainty. Three strangers. Three potential liabilities. Three people he'd have to trust with his life.

The pattern-sight couldn't tell him if they were trustworthy. It could only show him the shape of what they might build together.

*Sarah's voice, distant: "You can't do everything alone."*

"I'm in," he said.

Leon smiled. Not triumphant. Relieved.

"Then let's get to work."

## Notes After Writing

**What worked**:
- Each crew member introduced with distinct trait and voice
- Katya: systems-thinker, precise, reserved
- Dominik: protector, simple in best sense, kind
- Leon: coordinator, sees people's needs
- The "no profit" twist establishes stakes
- Sarah echo as decision-driver

**What needs testing**:
- Is Dominik too simple/nice? Does he need edge?
- Does the Robin Hood pitch feel earned or preachy?
- Is there enough tension in the assembly?
- Should someone almost walk away?

**Questions for next draft**:
- How do crew members know Leon?
- What are their individual backstories?
- Where does this fall in the G-arc? (Early Act 1?)
- Should ARCHITECT's threat feel more present?
