# Test Scene: Low Point (Variation G)

**Testing**: Does distrust-as-self-destruction work? Is crew fracture believable?
**Length**: ~1,000 words
**Status**: First draft complete

---

## The Scene

"It was one of us."

Elias's words dropped into the safehouse like stones into still water. Leon stopped mid-sentence. Katya's fingers froze over her keyboard. Dominik, who'd been cleaning the cut above his eye, looked up with something approaching hurt in his expression.

"What did you say?" Leon's voice was dangerously quiet.

"The tip-off. Someone told ARCHITECT's people we were coming. The timing was too precise—they knew exactly when we'd be in that server room. That means someone on this team talked."

"And you think it was one of us."

"I *know* it was one of us. That's how patterns work." Elias pulled up the timeline on his tablet, the data points he'd been obsessing over since they'd gotten back. "Look. The security shift change was accelerated by twelve minutes. The patrol pattern was adjusted to converge on our entry point. These aren't coincidences—they're responses to specific intelligence."

The room was silent.

"You've been analyzing us," Katya said flatly. "Since the job."

"I've been analyzing everything. That's what I do."

"No." Katya stood, and for the first time, Elias saw genuine anger in her precise features. "What you do is look for threats. And now you've decided we're the threat."

"I'm looking for the truth."

"You're looking for an excuse." Leon's voice cut through the tension. "An excuse to not trust us. To go back to being alone, because alone is safer. Alone doesn't betray you."

"This isn't about me."

"It's always about you." Leon moved closer, and there was something in his posture that Elias hadn't seen before—not menace, but disappointment. "You think I haven't seen this pattern? The talented loner who joins a team, starts to believe, and then the moment something goes wrong, decides that trust was a mistake?"

"Something *did* go wrong. We got made."

"And we got out. Together. Because that's what crews do." Leon shook his head. "But you can't see that, can you? All you can see is the part where your control slipped."

Elias felt something tighten in his chest. "That's not—"

"It's exactly what this is. You're not investigating a mole. You're sabotaging yourself before we can hurt you."

The tablet felt heavy in Elias's hands. The patterns were there, clear as daylight. The timing, the adjustments, the precise nature of the tip-off. Someone had talked.

But Leon was looking at him like he'd already lost something. And Katya was packing her laptop with sharp, angry movements. And Dominik—Dominik was just sitting there, the cut on his head still bleeding, watching Elias with those sad, steady eyes.

"I put my body between you and six armed guards," Dominik said quietly. "I did that because Leon asked me to, and because I believed you were worth protecting. Now you're telling me I'm a suspect?"

"I'm not—"

"Yes. You are." Dominik stood. He was huge, but in that moment, he seemed diminished somehow. "I understand suspicion. I understand caution. But this? This is you breaking what we built because you're afraid it might break on its own."

The words hit harder than a physical blow. Elias opened his mouth to respond—to defend, to explain, to justify—and found nothing.

Because Dominik was right.

The pattern-sight could find threats anywhere. It was designed to, optimized to, built for paranoia masquerading as intelligence. And he'd turned it on the only people who'd stood by him since Sarah died.

"I'm sorry." The words came out rough, broken. "I don't—I don't know how to do this."

"Do what?" Leon asked.

"Trust. Real trust. Not the kind where you calculate the odds and decide the risk is acceptable. The kind where you just... believe in people."

Leon's expression shifted—the disappointment softening into something that might have been understanding. "Nobody knows how to do that. You learn it. By doing it wrong, over and over, until you start doing it right."

"And if I can't?"

"Then you leave. Right now. Walk out that door and go back to drinking yourself to death in Eastern European bars." Leon's eyes were steady. "But if you stay, you stop analyzing us. You stop looking for betrayal. You accept that sometimes things go wrong, and it's not because someone failed you—it's just because the world is chaos and crews adapt."

Elias looked at the tablet. At the patterns that might be evidence and might be paranoia and probably were both.

He set it down.

"I'm staying."

"You're sure?"

"No." He managed something that was almost a laugh. "I'm never sure of anything. But I'm choosing to stay anyway."

Katya stopped packing. Dominik's face relaxed. Leon nodded slowly, the thin smile returning.

"Good. Then let's figure out what actually happened, together, without accusations." He picked up the tablet. "Show me your data. If there really is a leak, we find it as a team."

"And if there isn't?"

"Then we find out what we missed. That's what crews do." Leon handed the tablet back. "Together."

Elias took it. The patterns were still there, still demanding interpretation. But for the first time, he let himself consider that maybe he was the one who'd gotten it wrong.

Maybe that was okay.

## Notes After Writing

**What worked**:
- Crew confronting Elias's distrust as self-sabotage
- Dominik's "I put my body between you" as guilt-hammer
- "Sabotaging yourself before we can hurt you" as diagnosis
- Elias admitting he doesn't know how to trust
- "Together" as resolution without dismissing the real problem

**What needs testing**:
- Is the crew's forgiveness too quick?
- Should one of them actually be angrier longer?
- Is the actual mole question resolved too easily?
- Does Elias's breakdown feel earned?

**Questions for next draft**:
- Is there actually a mole? (Or was it something else?)
- Should this lead to investigating an external leak?
- Does Katya need more voice in this scene?
- How does this connect to the climax?
