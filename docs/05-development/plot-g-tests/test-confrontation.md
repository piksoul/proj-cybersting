# Test Scene: Confrontation (Variation G)

**Testing**: Does crew-trust vs control work in crisis? Is the antagonist presence felt?
**Length**: ~1,100 words
**Status**: First draft complete

---

## The Scene

They'd been made.

Elias saw it in the pattern before anyone else—the way the security guard's hand moved to his earpiece, the sudden stiffness in his shoulders, the micro-glance toward the camera that meant someone was talking in his ear, telling him things.

"Leon. We have a problem."

"I see it." Leon's voice was calm in his earpiece, maddeningly calm. "Katya, can you loop the feeds?"

"Already done. They're not looking at cameras. Someone tipped them off."

The server room felt smaller now, the hum of the machines louder. Elias had seven minutes of access time left. Seven minutes to extract the data that would map ARCHITECT's financial network, the proof they needed for the next phase.

Seven minutes that had just become a lot more complicated.

"Options," Leon said.

"I can finish." Elias's fingers hovered over the keyboard. "Four more minutes, maybe five."

"You don't have four minutes. Security's moving."

"Then buy me time."

Silence on the comms. Then Dominik's voice, low and certain: "I can do it."

"No." Elias's response was immediate, reflexive. "We stick to the plan. Everyone out, I'll—"

"The plan is gone." Leon's voice cut through like a blade. "We adapt or we lose. Dominik, can you give us three minutes?"

"I can give you four."

"Then give us three and get out clean. Katya, prep the extraction route. Elias—" A pause. "Do you trust us?"

The question hung in the air. Elias stared at the screen, the progress bar crawling, the pattern-sight screaming at him to take control, to find a way to manage every variable, to not depend on people he'd known for six weeks.

Sarah's voice, unbidden: *"The rest of us know you have to trust people too."*

"Yes," he said. "I trust you."

"Then finish the job. We'll get you out."

---

The next four minutes were the longest of Elias's life.

He worked blind—no visibility on Dominik's position, no way to know what Katya was doing to the security systems, nothing but Leon's occasional status updates and the faith that they knew what they were doing.

"Contact," Dominik said. His voice was steady. "Two guards, east corridor."

"Can you—"

"Handling it."

Sounds in Elias's earpiece—scuffling, a grunt, something heavy hitting the floor. Then silence, and Dominik's breathing, slightly faster but still controlled.

"Clear. Moving to secondary position."

"Two minutes," Leon said. "Elias, status?"

"Sixty percent. Maybe ninety seconds to completion."

"You have ninety seconds."

Elias's hands flew across the keyboard. The pattern-sight mapped the data structure, finding the paths of least resistance, the fastest routes to what they needed. He could see it all—the beautiful terrible architecture of ARCHITECT's network, the way money flowed like water through channels designed to be invisible.

"Security's converging on your floor," Katya said. "Six personnel. Armed."

"Dominik?"

"I can't hold six."

"You don't have to. Just slow them down." Leon's voice shifted—harder now, the voice of command. "Elias, you have thirty seconds."

"I need forty-five."

"You have thirty."

The progress bar crept forward. Elias watched it, willing it to move faster, fighting the urge to abort, to run, to take control of the only thing he could control—his own survival.

*Trust them.*

"Twenty seconds. Security's entering the east stairwell."

"Extraction route's clear," Katya said. "But the window's closing. Fifteen seconds, maybe twenty."

"Dominik, fall back."

"If I fall back, they're on Elias in sixty seconds."

"Then fall back in sixty seconds."

"Copy."

The bar hit ninety percent. Ninety-five.

Elias heard Dominik moving, heard the guards shouting, heard Katya's fingers on keys and Leon's breathing in his ear. The crew. His crew. Risking everything so he could finish the job.

Ninety-eight. Ninety-nine.

Complete.

"Done. I'm out."

"Move. Now. West corridor, third door on your left, maintenance shaft to sublevel. Katya's waiting."

He moved. Didn't think. Just trusted.

---

The van was waiting two blocks away, engine running. Elias dove into the back, and Katya hit the gas before the door was fully closed. Dominik was already inside, bleeding from a cut on his temple, grinning like a man who'd just won a prize fight.

"Told you I could hold them."

"You're an idiot," Katya said, but there was warmth in it.

Leon turned from the passenger seat, studying Elias with those sharp, assessing eyes. "You had the data. You could have aborted when we got made. You didn't."

"You told me to trust you."

"I did. And you did." Leon smiled—the thin, knowing expression that Elias was starting to recognize as genuine approval. "That's progress."

Elias slumped against the van's wall, adrenaline fading, reality crashing in. They'd made it. Somehow, against every pattern he could read, they'd made it.

"Who tipped them off?" he asked.

"Good question. We'll figure it out." Leon's smile faded. "ARCHITECT knows someone's targeting him now. The next job will be harder."

"Then we get harder."

Dominik laughed—a booming sound that filled the van. "I like him. Can we keep him?"

"That's up to him." Leon's eyes stayed on Elias. "So? Are you in? Really in? Not just for the job, but for what comes after?"

Elias thought about the server room. About the moment he'd chosen to keep working instead of running. About the crew that had gotten him out when everything went wrong.

"I'm in."

"Good." Leon turned back to face the road. "Because this is just the beginning."

## Notes After Writing

**What worked**:
- Ticking clock creates pressure for trust decision
- Dominik's physical sacrifice makes trust concrete
- "Do you trust us?" as explicit turning point
- Crew coordination through comms (auditory tension)
- Post-job debrief shows crew dynamics

**What needs testing**:
- Is the "tipped off" mystery enough antagonist presence?
- Should we see ARCHITECT's voice/representative?
- Is Dominik's action too heroic?
- Does the van escape feel too clean?

**Questions for next draft**:
- Who tipped them off? (Setup for later reveal)
- Is this mid-book or later Act 2?
- Should there be more physical action?
- Does Elias's trust-turn feel earned?
