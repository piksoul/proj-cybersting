# Test Scene: Opening Hook (Variation G)

**Testing**: Does the grief-to-recruitment hook work? Is Elias's state compelling?
**Length**: ~1,000 words
**Status**: First draft complete

## The Scene

Three months after Sarah died, Elias stopped running.

Not because he'd given up. Because he'd run out of places that didn't remind him of her.

The bar in Kraków was supposed to be anonymous—dim lights, sticky floors, the kind of place where people came to disappear into their drinks. Elias had found it through a contact who owed him a favor, and he'd been nursing the same whiskey for two hours, watching the door and waiting for nothing in particular.

The pattern-sight never stopped. Even now, even drunk, he could see the rhythm of the room—the bartender's tells when someone was about to start trouble, the couple in the corner whose body language said affair not marriage, the man by the window who'd been watching Elias since he walked in.

He'd noticed that last one immediately. Hard not to. The man wasn't trying to hide.

Elias finished his whiskey and set the glass down carefully. "You going to come over, or should I start guessing who sent you?"

The man smiled—a thin, knowing expression—and crossed the room. He moved like someone who'd spent decades learning exactly how much space his body occupied and how to use every inch of it.

"Nobody sent me," he said, sliding into the opposite seat. "I came looking."

"For me specifically, or just anyone who matches my description?"

"For Elias Richter. The pattern-reader who disappeared three months ago after his girlfriend died in what the police called an accident." The man's eyes were sharp, assessing. "I know it wasn't an accident. So do you."

Elias's hand tightened on the empty glass. "Who are you?"

"Leon. Just Leon." He signaled the bartender—two more whiskeys, top shelf—without breaking eye contact. "And I have a proposition for you."

"I'm not interested in propositions."

"You're not interested in anything right now. That's the problem." Leon accepted the drinks, slid one across the table. "You're sitting in a bar in Kraków, watching a door that nobody's going to walk through, waiting for something to matter enough to make you move. How long are you planning to do that?"

"Until I figure out who killed Sarah."

"And when you figure that out?"

"Then I kill them."

Leon nodded slowly, like this was exactly what he'd expected to hear. "What if I told you I know who killed her? And what if I told you that killing them isn't enough?"

The pattern-sight flared. Elias read Leon's face—the micro-expressions, the tension in his shoulders, the way his pupils didn't dilate when he said Sarah's name. Not lying. Not manipulating. Offering.

"I'm listening."

"There's a man who calls himself ARCHITECT. You won't find his real name—I've been looking for fifteen years. What you'll find is his work: money laundering on a global scale, funding for the worst people you can imagine, a network that touches everything from weapons trafficking to human exploitation. He doesn't get his hands dirty. He just makes sure the money flows to the people who do."

"And Sarah?"

"Sarah found something. I don't know exactly what—her research was scattered, encrypted, spread across systems I haven't been able to crack. But I know ARCHITECT ordered her death. She was getting too close to something he couldn't afford to have exposed."

Elias stared at the whiskey. The amber liquid caught the dim light, glowing like something alive.

"Why come to me?"

"Because I'm putting together a crew. People with specific skills, specific motivations. I need someone who can see patterns—in data, in systems, in the way people move through the world. Someone who can map ARCHITECT's network and find the weak points."

"You want me to help you take him down?"

"I want you to help me *rob* him. Take the money he's been laundering and give it back to the people it was stolen from. Destroy his network from the inside, piece by piece, until there's nothing left."

"Robin Hood."

"Something like that." Leon smiled again, but this time there was warmth in it. "It won't bring Sarah back. Nothing will. But it might give you something better than revenge."

"What's better than revenge?"

"Purpose. A crew. People who have your back instead of a bottle that doesn't care if you live or die."

The words hit harder than they should have. Elias had been alone for three months—alone in his grief, alone in his rage, alone in the endless circling of a mind that couldn't stop looking for patterns in a death that shouldn't have happened.

"Why should I trust you?"

"You shouldn't. Not yet. Trust is earned, not given." Leon finished his whiskey and stood. "But I'll be at this address tomorrow at noon. If you show up, I'll introduce you to the crew. If you don't, I'll find another pattern-reader and you can go back to drinking yourself to death in Eastern European bars."

He set a card on the table—plain white, an address handwritten in neat black ink.

"The choice is yours, Elias. But Sarah would tell you to stop moping and do something useful with your pain."

Elias's head snapped up. "You didn't know her."

"No. But I know people like her. People who see things clearly and act on what they see." Leon's voice softened. "She was one of the rare ones. And she would hate what you're doing to yourself right now."

He walked out without waiting for a response.

Elias sat in the dim bar, the card in one hand and the whiskey in the other. The pattern-sight mapped the room, the exits, the odds.

Tomorrow at noon.

He had until then to decide whether he wanted to live or just keep waiting to die.

## Notes After Writing

**What worked**:
- Bar as limbo state (waiting for nothing)
- Leon reading Elias before Elias reads him
- "Trust is earned, not given" as theme setup
- "Stop moping and do something useful" as Sarah-voice
- The card as physical choice

**What needs testing**:
- Is three months the right time gap?
- Does Leon's pitch feel too smooth?
- Should we see more of Elias's grief before Leon appears?
- Is the Sarah connection clear enough for opener?

**Questions for next draft**:
- How much does Elias know about ARCHITECT already?
- Should Leon mention the other crew members?
- Does the Robin Hood pitch need more specifics?
- Is this the right first scene, or should we see Sarah alive first?
