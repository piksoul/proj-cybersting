## 1. Baum's Bankruptcy — The Allegorist Undone

**Reference:** L. Frank Baum, author of *The Wizard of Oz* (1900), filed for bankruptcy in 1911 for $12,600. The man who wrote America's most famous monetary allegory was destroyed by a sum that today wouldn't register as a rounding error in the systems we're writing about.

**Location in story:** Early first act — establishing world/tone

**Placement:** Background detail or character speech (could work as sardonic observation from a cynical character, or as something Elias reads/thinks)

**The Drop:**

> "The man who wrote the most famous monetary allegory in American literature — silver shoes, gold roads, the fraud behind the curtain — was bankrupted by an amount that wouldn't cover a single month's gas fees during a busy period on Ethereum."

**Alternative versions:**

> "Baum wrote *The Wizard of Oz* as a parable about monetary policy. Silver shoes, gold roads, the humbug behind the curtain. He went bankrupt for twelve thousand dollars. Today that's dust. Less than dust. That's what gets lost in the spread."

> "You know Baum died still writing Oz books? Tried to end the series, went broke on a multimedia venture, had to crawl back to the franchise. The man who told everyone 'the power was with you all along' lost the rights to his own story for twenty years."

**Thematic resonance:**
- Creators aren't exempt from the systems they critique
- The Spectacle consumes its creators
- Historical irony reinforces our themes about monetary illusion
- Sets sardonic, knowing tone

**Who might say it:**
- A cynical analyst character
- Elias in internal monologue
- Background media/podcast playing
- A character explaining why they distrust "visionaries"
