# Style Seeds — Prose Agent Reference

> **Usage:** When a scene needs specific texture (a track playing, a brand mentioned, a visual reference), draw from this doc. The core rule is scarcity — named references are load-bearing and must earn their place.

---

## The Namedropping Rule

**Every named reference must do narrative work the scene couldn't do otherwise.**

Signs a scene has slipped into liner-notes mode:
- Multiple tracks named in quick succession
- Characters commenting on the music ("oh this one")
- Track credits appearing in narration without doing narrative work
- Brand names used for tribal signalling without purpose
- A "playlist" or "catalogue" feeling — the reader starts keeping score

**The test for a named reference:** if you removed the name and replaced it with sensory description, would the scene lose something essential? If not, the name doesn't belong there.

**Rule of thumb:**
- **One named music track per scene, maximum**
- **Most scenes: zero named tracks** — ambient description only
- **Recurring motif tracks** appear twice across the novel, three at the ceiling, sometimes once is enough

## Music Motifs

### Named Tracks with Narrative Function

#### "Don't Sweat the Technique" — Eric B. & Rakim (1992)

- **Scene:** Ch1 Scene 1 — The Training Space
- **Function:** Title-to-action rhyme. Plays at the moment Elias commits early and nails the precision landing he's been fumbling. The title becomes the thing he's doing.
- **Thematic weight:** Rakim's MC philosophy is about precision, flow, and making technique disappear into instinct. Leon's teaching is the same. Elias has to learn to stop sweating the technique to achieve the flow state that quiets the pattern-sight.
- **Usage:** Once, precisely placed. Not referenced again.
- **Caution:** Eric B. & Rakim aren't canonical breaker tracks (breakers traditionally train to funk breaks). Using them here is a specific taste signal — Leon's European golden-age hip-hop palette, not the foundational breaker canon. This is deliberate.

#### "It's Just Begun" — Jimmy Castor Bunch (1972)

- **Held for:** The climax (or the Act 2→3 transition into the climax)
- **Function:** Second-wind track. Canonical breaker anthem — the riff that inspires movement from the first note. Title carries literal foreshadow: after everything, after all the loss, *it's just begun*.
- **Usage plan:**
  1. **First use (diegetic):** Plays for real in a quiet moment before the final push. Possibly the crew training one last time, or a private reset scene. Leon puts it on. Elias commits to the sequence and the decision simultaneously.
  2. **Second use (internal):** Plays in Elias's head at the climax itself. Alone under pressure, the drum break is his metronome. The body remembers the rhythm of commitment.
- **Caution:** Two uses maximum. This is the track with the highest thematic load — it must be protected from overuse.

### Unnamed Music Texture (Describe Sensorially)

When music is present but shouldn't be named:

- **Training ambient (early session):** *"a funk break from the oldest section of Leon's rotation,"* *"bass frequencies more felt than heard,"* *"the steady pulse of something with a crate-dug drum line"*
- **Wind-down transition (end of session):** Leon changes to **dub** — *"the low, reverb-heavy bass of whatever Leon put on when the session was over,"* *"dub now, patient and dragged through echo chambers,"* *"the kind of music that ended a night without announcing anything"*
- **Scene 2 after-hours:** *"an ambient track cycling into something slower, something without percussion, that hung in the air like a question"* (can keep the "question" line from the original output)

## Visual / Art References

### Graffiti and Street Art (Berlin)

- **The training space walls:** layers of tags over tags over tags. The oldest "geological now, their colours bled into the mortar." Specific styles available:
  - Old-school throw-ups and pieces from the 90s/2000s (preserved underneath)
  - Newer stickers and tags
  - One or two pieces by recognisable (fictional) local writers
  - Political stencils from specific Berlin movements
- **Berlin street texture:** wheat-pasted posters for underground parties, political graffiti, gallery-quality work in unexpected corners, the usual tagging war

**Agent rule:** Describe the layering and content sensorially. Don't name specific real artists. If a signature matters, invent one that sounds authentic.

### Interior / Atmosphere

- **Elias's apartment:** sparse, functional, one bookshelf of things he couldn't bear to throw away. A few objects from his old life that he doesn't look at. No photos on the walls.
- **Training space lighting:** yellow work lights on tripod stands (Dominik's arrangement), ceiling lost in shadow seven metres up, natural light only through high dirty windows during day sessions
- **Berlin at night:** sodium streetlights, neon from kebab shops and late bars, the blue glow of S-Bahn platforms, wet cobblestones catching light

## Tech / Object Specificity

When describing technology, gadgets, or objects, lean on the **real-world analogue** rule (see `ref-tech-bible.md`) and avoid inventing brand names when real ones would serve.

- **Phones:** unnamed but specific in behaviour ("his phone sat on the kitchen counter, dark")
- **Laptops/terminals:** "a black terminal window," "the blue glow of monitors" — avoid naming brands unless a specific brand signals something about the character
- **Burner hardware:** described by function ("a burner phone," "a prepaid SIM") — brand-agnostic

## The "Something" Audit

A scene has failed the specificity test if it contains more than one instance of "something [adjective]" as a hedge. Specific replacements:

| Lazy | Specific |
|------|----------|
| "something low and repetitive" | "a funk break, 93 bpm, all kick and snare" |
| "something slower, without percussion" | "an ambient track, dub-adjacent, bass only" |
| "cups of something" | "cups of Leon's black tea," "mate in enamel cups" |
| "something about the way he moved" | actual description of what the movement showed |
| "something unspoken" | what *specifically* is unspoken, named |

**Agent rule:** Reach for a specific before reaching for "something." If no specific exists in the reference docs, ask the author rather than hedging.
