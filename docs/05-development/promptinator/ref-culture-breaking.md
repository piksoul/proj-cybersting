# Breaking Culture — Prose Agent Reference

> **Usage:** Ground reference for any scene set in the Berlin training space or the wider breaking world. Authentic details replace generic "training montage" texture. Getting this right is how the reader trusts that the story knows its world.

---

## Core Rule: No Chalk

**Breakers do NOT use chalk.**

This is the opposite of parkour, climbing, and gymnastics. Breakers need their hands to *slide* on concrete for spins, freezes, hand-spins, and floor moves. Chalk would ruin the surface interaction.

**Rituals in place of chalk:**
- A sip from a water bottle (ritual reset between attempts)
- Wiping palms on thighs or loose clothing
- A sharp exhale and shoulder roll before committing
- Sometimes a rub of fingertips against the floor to test surface grip
- Rhythmic bouncing on the balls of the feet before entering the circle

**Agent rule:** Never write a breaker chalking up. If a reset ritual is needed, use one of the above.

## Greeting Hierarchy

Breakers have a codified social greeting system. Use the right tier for the relationship.

| Tier | Form | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| **1. Nod** | Chin lift, brief eye contact | Minimum acknowledgment. Crew knows each other. |
| **2. Fist bump** | Casual knuckle touch | Friendly. Passing greeting. Low energy, no ceremony. |
| **3. Full dap** | Complex handshake → grip → finger flick follow-through | Full welcome. Done with respect, full attention. |

**Cultural notes:**
- The full dap is *earned* between equals. It's not performed casually.
- A nod can be warmer than a dap depending on how it's given.
- Papa (the crew elder) does the full dap with everyone, including strangers — it's his signature discipline, not a measure of relationship.
- Elias gets a **hand on the shoulder** from Papa instead of a dap. This is non-ritual, special, never explained.

## The Introvert Default

**Breakers as a species are naturally shy, introverted, and a bit standoffish.** Default behaviour around new crews or strangers:

- Circle the edges before joining in
- Watch before committing
- Prove themselves through movement before speaking
- Default to "battle mode" (wary, competitive stance) when around a new crew
- Self-conscious about training or failing in front of others they don't know

**This is the cultural ground that makes the Old Guy (Papa) matter.** He swims against this tide deliberately. His handshake discipline is cultural work — bringing the shy species into a space where they're seen.

**Katya is the exception, not the rule.** She's battle-wired — hostile rather than shy. Most breakers are the other kind.

**Elias fits in easily** because his natural reticence reads as *respect* rather than aloofness. He doesn't push, doesn't perform, doesn't ask questions. That's how you earn a place.

**Blow-in breakers** (travelers passing through, visiting crews) need managing. They arrive guarded, test the circle quietly, accept or reject the space within the first session. Papa's handshake ritual is explicitly aimed at them — a signal that says *you're welcome even if you don't know anyone here*.

## Circle Etiquette

- Wait your turn — the circle moves organically, don't force your way in
- If you enter, commit. Half-attempts are disrespectful to the circle.
- Failures are acknowledged with nods, fist bumps, or laughter — never ignored, never pitied
- A killer move gets audible reaction (yells, exclamations, specific hand signals)
- After someone else's turn, nod or tap them as they exit — acknowledgment
- Don't coach mid-circle unless asked
- Watching from the edge is normal — you're not expected to participate every moment

## Training Space Layout

Typical elements of a breaker training space:

- **Marked zone** — the cipher/circle area, usually taped or chalk-lined on the floor (this chalk is for the floor, not the hands)
- **Wall** — used for wall-runs, tricks, stretching against
- **Speaker(s)** — usually portable, placed at the edge. Whoever owns the speaker usually controls the playlist.
- **Mats** — stacked against walls when not in use, pulled out for specific moves
- **Water bottles scattered** — everywhere, personal
- **Layers of clothing** — arriving cold, training warm, leaving cold again. Visible markers of session phase.
- **Worn landing zones** — the concrete or floor surface wears distinct patterns where years of practice have compressed the surface

## Movement Vocabulary (Use Sparingly)

Technical terms exist but should be deployed with restraint. The reader doesn't need a glossary — they need to feel the movement.

| Term | Meaning | When to use in prose |
|------|---------|---------------------|
| **Freeze** | A held balance/pose, usually on hands or head | Common, reader-friendly |
| **Top-rock** | Standing-level footwork before going down | Common, reader-friendly |
| **Down-rock** | Floor-level footwork | Use sparingly |
| **Power move** | Dynamic spinning moves (windmill, flare, air-flare) | Refer generally, rarely by name |
| **Precision** (parkour term) | Landing exactly on a target | Use for commitment moments |
| **Flow** | Uninterrupted sequence; the state of being inside the movement | Thematically central — use deliberately |
| **The cipher** | The circle where dancers trade turns | Use when appropriate |
| **Battle** | Competitive face-off | Save for scenes where it matters |

**Agent rule:** Describe movement sensorially first, technically second. *"He landed clean, shoulders dropping as the weight transferred through his centre"* beats *"He executed a precision landing from the jump phase of the sequence."*

## Elias's Specific Constraints

His frozen shoulder shapes what he does and doesn't do in the circle:

- **Avoids:** deep hand freezes, air-flares, any move that loads the shoulder joint under rotation
- **Favours:** footwork-heavy sequences, precision work (parkour-adjacent), wall-runs, controlled falls
- **Half-beat hesitation in commitment landings** — he's unconsciously protecting the joint
- **His sequence in Scene 1** — a flow through a jump to a precision to a wall-run to a controlled fall — is deliberately shoulder-friendly
- When the shoulder tightens, certain moves come off the menu entirely. The crew knows (without him telling them).

## Thematic Resonance

Breaking and parkour in this story carry thematic weight that goes beyond sport:

- **Flow state** parallels the emergence — collective movement the whole is greater than the sum of
- **Circle etiquette** parallels decentralised consensus — rules without rulers
- **Personal proof of work** — the phrase Elias uses for his training; directly mirrors Bitcoin's proof-of-work mechanism
- **Trust in the body** — the one system Elias has learnt to trust after all others failed him
- **The discipline of showing up** — Papa's handshake work, the crew's continuing presence, the circle that holds because people keep returning

These resonances should emerge from action and observation, never from explicit commentary.
