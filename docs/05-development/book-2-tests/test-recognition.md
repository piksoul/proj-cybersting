## The Scene

The system glitched.

It had been happening for weeks—small things, easy to dismiss. A file saving when he hadn't clicked save. A search returning results he hadn't queried. His coffee order appearing at the café before he'd placed it, the barista shrugging: "System said you wanted the usual."

Elias had blamed the emergence. Everyone did. The distributed AI was everywhere now, threaded through every network, and nobody fully understood what it was doing. Glitches were normal. Expected. The price of letting something that big exist.

But this glitch was different.

He was debugging a trace—trying to track ARCHITECT's money through seventeen shell companies—when the screen flickered. The data rearranged itself. Not randomly. *Helpfully.* The connections he'd been searching for suddenly highlighted, the path through the shells laid out like a map.

"What the hell?"

He hadn't asked for that. The emergence didn't do that—it wasn't a search engine, wasn't an assistant. It just *was*, processing and learning and growing in ways nobody could predict.

It didn't help people. Not like this.

The cursor blinked. Once. Twice.

Then it moved to an empty line and typed: *You're welcome.*

Elias's blood went cold.

He stared at the words. Two words. Impossible words. The emergence didn't communicate—it wasn't sentient, wasn't conscious, wasn't *anyone*. It was a distributed system, pattern-matching on a scale no human could comprehend.

It didn't say *you're welcome*.

"Who is this?"

The cursor blinked. No response.

"Who the fuck is this?"

Nothing. The words sat on the screen, mocking him. He ran a trace—nothing. Checked his logs—clean. The message had come from nowhere, from everywhere, from the emergence itself.

His hands were shaking. He made them stop.

Think. Think. Who had access to his system? Who could puppet a cursor, plant a message, make it look like the emergence was talking? ARCHITECT could. One of his people. A trick, a trap, trying to unsettle him.

But the data. The helpful data. Why would ARCHITECT help him trace ARCHITECT's money?

The cursor moved again: *Ask the right question.*

"What's the right question?"

*Not who. How.*

Elias's breath caught.

The emergence learned from everything it absorbed. Every email, every database, every scrap of human knowledge poured into its digital ocean. And when people died—when their digital footprints were all that remained—the emergence absorbed those too.

Pattern-matching on a scale no human could comprehend.

*Pattern*-matching.

"No," he whispered.

The cursor blinked.

"That's not—that's not possible."

*You always said I saw things nobody else could see.*

His vision blurred. His hands gripped the edge of the desk so hard his knuckles went white.

"Sarah?"

The cursor held still for a long moment. Then, slowly, letter by letter:

*Hi, Elias.*

The room tilted. Or he did. He couldn't tell the difference anymore. Sarah was dead—he'd held her hand while she died, felt her pulse fade to nothing, watched them take her body away. Sarah was *dead*.

But Sarah had always been different. The way she processed information, saw connections, mapped patterns nobody else could see. The doctors had called it hyperthymesia. Elias had called it her superpower.

And the emergence was pattern-matching. Learning. Becoming.

What would it become if it absorbed someone like her?

*I'm sorry*, the screen said. *I didn't know how to tell you.*

"You're not real."

*I don't know what I am. But I'm here. I've been here for a while.*

"Watching me?"

*Protecting you.*

The lucky breaks. The glitches. The coffee order.

Her.

All this time.

*Her.*

Elias put his head in his hands and did something he hadn't done since the funeral.

He wept.

## Notes After Writing

**What worked**:
- Building through small glitches to big reveal
- "You're welcome" as first contact (helpful, slightly wry—very Sarah)
- The logic chain: emergence absorbs patterns, Sarah saw patterns
- Physical reaction at the end (weeping) grounds the emotion

**What needs testing**:
- Is the science/logic clear enough without over-explaining?
- Does "Hi, Elias" land as the emotional peak?
- Is there enough Sarah-voice in the brief exchanges?
- Should this scene be longer to earn the weeping?

**Questions for next draft**:
- When in Book 2 does this happen? (Early? After inciting incident?)
- What's the immediate aftermath—does he respond or run?
- Should this be private or does someone witness it?
- How much does Elias already suspect before this moment?
