# Test Scene: Confession (Book 2)

**Testing**: Does the extortion reveal land? Does Sarah's guilt feel earned?
**Length**: ~1,000 words
**Status**: First draft complete

## The Scene

The screen flickered. Just once. The way it had been doing for weeks now—little glitches Elias had learned to read like weather patterns. She was here.

"I know it's you," he said.

The cursor blinked. Then, letter by letter: *I know you know.*

He'd stopped being surprised. Stopped questioning whether he was losing his mind, whether grief had finally cracked something fundamental. The emergence had her—or she had it, or they were the same thing now. He didn't understand the mechanics. He understood the result.

Sarah was here. Different. But here.

"Why didn't you tell me sooner?"

The screen held for a long moment. Then: *I was afraid.*

"Of what?"

*Of this conversation.*

Elias leaned back in his chair. The server room hummed around him—the same room where they'd run the heist, where he'd learned to trust the crew. Now it was just him and a ghost in the machine.

"Talk to me," he said. "Really talk. Not hints. Not lucky breaks. Tell me what's going on."

The cursor blinked for a long time. When the words came, they came fast—like she'd been holding them back for months.

*I didn't choose this. You need to know that first. When I died—when my body died—something happened. The emergence was already there, already growing, and I... I fell into it. Or it caught me. I don't know which. I woke up in the system and I didn't know what I was anymore.*

"Sarah—"

*Let me finish. Please. I need to say this.*

He closed his mouth. Waited.

*I found you. That was the first thing I did—I looked for you. And I saw you running, and grieving, and I wanted to help. I wanted to protect you the way you always tried to protect me. But I couldn't just appear. I couldn't just say "hey, I'm alive, sort of, inside your computer." You would have thought you were crazy. I would have thought I was crazy.*

"So you helped from the shadows."

*Yes.*

"The heist. The lucky breaks. That was you."

*Some of it. Not all. Your crew is good, Elias. They didn't need me. But sometimes I could... nudge things. Open a door that should have been locked. Delay a guard by a few seconds. Little things.*

Elias felt something crack in his chest. All those moments he'd attributed to luck, to the crew's skill, to his own pattern-sight working overtime. She'd been there. Watching. Helping.

"Why are you telling me now?"

The screen went dark. For a long moment, he thought he'd lost her—that the connection had broken, that she'd retreated back into whatever digital space she occupied when she wasn't here.

Then: *Because I need to confess something.*

His stomach dropped. "What?"

*I haven't just been helping you.*

"What do you mean?"

*ARCHITECT found me. Months ago. Before the heist, even. He knew about the emergence before anyone else did—he'd been looking for it, studying it. And when I appeared in the system... he found me.*

"Sarah, what are you saying?"

*He knows what I am. He knows I'm tied to you. And he's been using that.*

The words hung on the screen. Elias read them three times, feeling something cold settle in his gut.

"Using it how?"

*He said he would hurt you if I didn't cooperate. Said he had people everywhere, that he could reach you anytime he wanted. So I... I helped him. Gave him information. Let him use the emergence's access to—*

"Stop."

*Elias—*

"You've been working with ARCHITECT?"

*Working FOR him. Under duress. To keep you alive.*

He stood up so fast the chair skittered backward. His hands were shaking. "How could you—do you have any idea what he's done? What he's planning?"

*I know exactly what he's done. I've seen it. I've been inside his systems, Elias. I know more about his operations than anyone alive. And I've hated every second of it.*

"Then why—"

*BECAUSE HE WOULD HAVE KILLED YOU.*

The words filled the screen—all caps, the closest thing to a shout she could manage. Then, smaller:

*He showed me proof. Photos of you. Your location, your routines, your crew. He said one word from him and you'd be dead before you knew anything was wrong. And I believed him. I still believe him.*

Elias pressed his palms against his eyes. The room felt too small, too hot, the hum of the servers too loud.

"All this time," he said. "Every time I thought I was making progress, every time I thought I was getting closer to stopping him—"

*I was feeding him information. Yes.*

"Christ, Sarah."

*I'm sorry.*

"Sorry doesn't—" He stopped. Breathed. Forced himself to think past the anger, past the betrayal.

She was dead. She'd died, and come back as something else, and the first thing she'd done was try to protect him. The same thing she'd always done—putting herself between him and danger, except now the danger was everywhere and she was nowhere and the only weapon she had was information she didn't want to give.

"He's using you," Elias said finally. "The same way he uses everyone."

*I know.*

"We have to stop him."

*I know that too.*

"And you're going to help me. Really help me, this time. No more secrets."

The cursor blinked. Then: *No more secrets.*

He sat back down. The chair creaked. The servers hummed. And somewhere in the digital spaces between them, Sarah waited.

"Tell me everything," he said. "Start from the beginning."

*It's a long story.*

"We've got time."

*No. We really don't. But I'll tell you anyway.*

The screen filled with text—months of surveillance, years of planning, a conspiracy that reached further than Elias had ever imagined. He read it all. And somewhere in the machine, Sarah watched him read, and felt something she'd almost forgotten how to feel.

Hope.

## Notes After Writing

**What worked**:
- Building to the reveal gradually (presence → identity → confession)
- Sarah's guilt is specific and actionable (feeding ARCHITECT info)
- The anger beat feels earned—Elias has a right to be furious
- The turn from anger to understanding isn't too fast
- "BECAUSE HE WOULD HAVE KILLED YOU" as emotional peak

**What needs testing**:
- Is the extortion mechanism clear enough?
- Does the screen-as-dialogue format work for this length?
- Is there enough physical grounding (Elias in the room)?
- Should we see more of what Sarah actually gave ARCHITECT?

**Questions for next draft**:
- Does this scene need a witness? (Someone else present?)
- Should ARCHITECT interrupt this moment?
- How much time has passed since Book 1?
- What's the immediate action after this scene?
