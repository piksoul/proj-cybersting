# Test Scene: Precision Sequence (Book 1 Climax)

**Testing**: Does crew coordination work? Does trust-as-skill land?
**Length**: Full scene (~1,500 words)
**Status**: First draft complete

---

## The Scene

The comms crackled. Three voices, three positions, one window.

"Katya, status." Leon's voice was flat. No tension. Like he was asking about the weather.

"Thirty seconds to access. Maybe forty."

"Dominik?"

"Two in the lobby. One by the service elevator. They haven't seen me."

Elias pressed his back against the server room wall, fingers hovering over the terminal. The pattern was there—he could see it, the way the system breathed, the gaps in the monitoring cycles. Twelve seconds between sweeps. Twelve seconds to initiate the transfer.

But he couldn't do this alone.

That was the thing. The whole fucking thing. He could see exactly what needed to happen, could trace the path like light through fiber optic, and none of it mattered if he didn't trust them to be where they said they'd be, doing what they said they'd do.

*Sarah's voice, unbidden: "Precision... Focus... Flow..."*

He pushed it down.

"Leon, I need the timing."

"I know."

"When you call it—"

"I know, Elias." A pause. "I've been doing this longer than you've been running."

Elias closed his eyes. The old pattern: do it yourself, trust no one, control every variable. It had kept him alive. It hadn't saved Sarah.

"Twenty seconds," Katya said. "They're cycling the authentication. I need you to hold."

"Holding."

Dominik's breath was steady in his ear. Somewhere three floors below, the man was pressed into shadow, counting guards, measuring angles. Elias had seen him move—precise, economical, like a machine. But machines didn't choose. Dominik chose. Every time.

"Elias." Leon's voice again. "When it comes, don't think. You taught me that—in the gym, when you'd overthink a route and fall. Don't think. Move."

"That was different."

"No. It wasn't."

The server hummed. Somewhere in its guts, the money sat—stolen twice over, from people who'd never see it again unless someone did something. Unless they did something.

"Fifteen seconds," Katya said.

Elias's finger found the key. One press. One moment of trust.

*"Precision... Focus... Flow..."*

"I'm in position," Dominik said. "Ready on your call."

Leon breathed. Elias could hear him centering, that thing he did before a session—finding the rhythm that wasn't his, the one that lived underneath everything.

"Here it comes," Leon said. "Five seconds. Four. Three."

Elias stopped thinking.

"Two. One. *Now.*"

---

He pressed the key.

The terminal flashed—green, amber, green again. The transfer protocol initiated, handshaking with systems he couldn't see, couldn't verify.

"Katya?"

"I see it. Authentication's cycling. Hold."

He held. His finger hovered over the abort. Old instinct. Kill it before it kills you.

"They're pinging the subnet," Katya said, voice still calm. "Expected. Ignore it."

"Ignore it?"

"Trust me."

Two words. The hardest words in any language.

"Dominik, status." Leon, checking positions.

"Still clear. Wait—movement by the east stairwell. Two more coming down."

Elias's stomach dropped. "Leon—"

"Stay on target. Dominik, can you hold?"

A pause. Then: "I can hold."

The terminal beeped. Amber. The authentication was stalling. Elias's pattern-sight screamed at him—something was wrong, something in the timing, a gap he couldn't—

"Katya, the handshake's failing."

"I see it. Rerouting through secondary. Give me ten seconds."

"We don't have ten seconds."

"Then give me eight."

He watched the screen. The progress bar stuttered, crawled, stopped.

*Trust her. Trust her. Trust—*

The bar moved. Green.

"We're through," Katya said. "Transfer initiating. Ninety seconds to completion."

Ninety seconds. An eternity.

---

"Contact," Dominik said. His voice hadn't changed. Still steady. Still precise. "They've seen the stairwell camera loop. They know something's wrong."

"Can you move?" Leon asked.

"Moving now."

Elias heard it through the comms—footsteps, fast but controlled. Dominik wasn't running. He was flowing. The way he'd taught Elias to move through a course: don't fight the obstacle, become the path around it.

"Sixty seconds," Elias said. "Dominik, we need sixty more seconds."

"You'll have them."

No hesitation. No doubt. Just certainty—the kind that came from years of training, from trusting your body, from knowing exactly what you could do.

The kind Elias had never had. Not about himself. Not about anyone.

Until now.

"They're in the stairwell," Dominik said. "I'm going loud."

A crash. Shouts. The comms dissolved into chaos—Dominik's breathing hard, a grunt of impact, something breaking.

"Dominik!"

"Forty-five seconds," Leon said. "Stay on target, Elias."

"He's—"

"He's doing his job. Do yours."

The terminal glowed. The progress bar crept forward. Elias watched it, hands shaking, and did absolutely nothing.

That was the hardest part.

---

"Thirty seconds," Katya said.

Dominik's comms had gone quiet. Just breathing. Heavy, but controlled.

"Dom?" Elias couldn't help it. "Talk to me."

"Busy." A grunt. "Twenty seconds is fine. Make it twenty."

"Fifteen," Katya said.

The bar crawled. Elias counted heartbeats. His own. The system's.

"Ten."

"Movement on your floor," Leon said. "Elias, you've got incoming."

Footsteps in the corridor. Close. Getting closer.

"Five."

He could run. He could abort. He could—

"Completion," Katya said. "Transfer complete. We're ghosts."

The screen went green. All green. The money—millions, stolen from thousands, laundered through systems designed to erase its origins—was gone. Redistributed. Scattered to accounts that would never be traced, belonging to people who'd lost everything.

"Clear," Dominik said. "Moving to extraction."

"Elias, move. Now."

He moved.

---

They met at the van three blocks away. Dominik was bleeding from a cut above his eye. Katya was already in the driver's seat, engine running. Leon stood by the door, watching Elias approach.

"You stayed on target," Leon said.

"Barely."

"Barely counts."

They climbed in. Katya pulled away—smooth, unhurried, just another van in a city full of vans.

Elias sat in the darkness, heart still hammering. The money was gone. Not to them—they'd get nothing from this, not a cent. That had been the deal. Robin Hood, Katya had called it. Take from the bastards, give to the victims.

It had worked.

*It had worked.*

Not because he'd controlled it. Not because he'd done it alone. Because he'd trusted them—Katya's hands on the keys, Dominik's body in the breach, Leon's voice in his ear.

Sarah's voice, one last time: *"Precision... Focus... Flow... and now."*

He hadn't said it. But he'd heard her. And somehow, impossibly, he thought she'd heard him too.

The crew saved him.

The way he couldn't save her.

## Notes After Writing

**What worked**:
- Leon's calm as counterweight to Elias's tension
- Sarah's line breaking through (established, returns naturally)
- The "don't think, move" callback to training
- Hearing the action through comms rather than showing it directly
- Dominik "going loud" creates tension without showing the fight
- The "barely counts" beat at the end

**What needs testing**:
- Is Dominik distinct enough? (He's mostly physical presence, not voice)
- Does the complication (authentication stall) feel earned?
- Is the pacing right? (Staccato throughout, or need more variation?)

**Questions for next draft**:
- Do we need a pre-scene beat establishing positions?
- Should we see Dominik's action directly, or is comms-only more tense?
- Where does ARCHITECT factor in? (Present? Arriving? Remote?)
- Should there be more internal thought, or keep it action-focused?
