## Reflection

**What works**: The manic energy of financial news struggling to process an event beyond its frameworks. The social media crawl creates distributed voices—some panicked, some opportunistic, some oblivious. The breakdown of the broadcast mirrors the breakdown of the system.

**Satirical targets hit**:
- Financial media's addiction to spectacle
- The inability of news to explain what it's reporting
- Social media as collective consciousness/collective hysteria
- Government opacity at critical moments
- The Elon-shaped hole in any crypto narrative

**Questions raised**:
1. **What actually happens next?** The broadcast doesn't know—neither does anyone. What fills that vacuum?
2. **How does Spectacle consume its own failure?** Even the catastrophe becomes content.
3. **Where is Elias during this?** Watching? Running? Already ahead of the story?
4. **What does "the hash doesn't match" mean to the public?** Technical failure as narrative crisis.
5. **Who benefits from the chaos?** Not the Treasury, but someone...
6. **Does the Spectacle survive this, or does this break it?** Can media infrastructure handle genuine uncertainty?
7. **What happens to ordinary people watching this?** The 401k question is real—stakes beyond the thriller plot.

**Technical note**: The format (broadcast) allows exposition through "experts" without feeling like author info-dump. The glitches and interruptions create suspense through form, not just content.
