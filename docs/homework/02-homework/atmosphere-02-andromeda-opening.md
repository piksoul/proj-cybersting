# Control Room Breach - Andromeda Style Opening

## TREASURY CYBER OPERATIONS CENTER - SUBLEVEL 3

The Faraday cage hums at 60 Hz—a sound felt more than heard, vibrating through the rubberized floor tiles into the bones of anyone standing inside. Fourteen monitors arranged in a curved wall display real-time blockchain telemetry. Transaction hashes scroll like green rain. The center monitor—larger, isolated by six inches of dead space—shows only a blinking amber cursor.

TECH SERGEANT WILLIAMS adjusts a gain dial on the Cryptographic Anomaly Detection System. The CADS unit is a gray metal box the size of a filing cabinet, covered in warning stickers from three different administrations. One sticker, yellowed and curling: PROPERTY OF NSA - DO NOT SERVICE WITHOUT FORM 1142-C.

The dial clicks. Nothing changes on the screens.

---

## SECURE COMMUNICATIONS RELAY - ROOM 7

Four floors up, behind a door marked only with a number, DIRECTOR CHEN holds a telephone receiver built in 1987. The handset weighs nearly two pounds—deliberately. Weight implies security. The coiled cord stretches to a wall plate connected to nothing visible.

"Initiation sequence status?"

Static. Then: "Awaiting biometric confirmation from secondary."

Chen checks her watch. A Casio F-91W, the same model seized from detainees for two decades because of its timer function. She wears it for the irony. Or the reminder.

"Secondary is six minutes overdue."

"Acknowledged."

She hangs up. The receiver settles into its cradle with a mechanical chunk.

---

## SUBLEVEL 3 - CADS STATION

Williams leans closer to the anomaly detection readout. A single line of text has appeared beneath the scrolling hashes:

```
SIGNATURE VERIFICATION: WALLET 1A1zP1...
STATUS: PENDING
RETRY COUNT: 1
```

He has seen this screen for eleven years. He has never seen "RETRY COUNT."

His hand moves toward the internal phone—matte black, no dial pad, direct line only. Then stops.

The screen updates:

```
RETRY COUNT: 2
```

---

## CORRIDOR 7-A - EN ROUTE

AGENT MORRISON walks. Not runs—walking is protocol. Running indicates panic. Panic is a security breach of its own kind.

His shoes make no sound on the composite flooring. The corridor stretches forty meters, lit by panels that adjust automatically to eliminate shadows. Shadow elimination is mandated by Directive 2019-07: no surveillance blind spots in secure facilities.

He passes a fire extinguisher. A water fountain. An electrical panel.

Each object logged, catalogued, X-rayed weekly.

The door ahead requires three separate authentications. Morrison has two.

He keeps walking.

---

## SECURE COMMS - ROOM 7

Chen's phone does not ring. It illuminates—a red LED embedded in the receiver's base, silent and insistent.

She lifts it.

"The signature failed."

A pause. The weight of the handset suddenly feels heavier.

"Specify."

"Private key authorization returned invalid. CADS logged it as a signing fault, but—" The voice hesitates. Hesitation is also not protocol. "—the keys were correct. The vault confirms physical integrity. The hardware is... arguing with itself."

Chen looks at the wall. Beige paint, twenty years old, four layers deep. Each layer a different administration. Each layer believing it controlled what happened in this building.

"Lock the floor. No one in or out. Including me."

She replaces the receiver.

The LED stays red.

---

## SUBLEVEL 3 - CADS STATION

```
RETRY COUNT: 7
RETRY COUNT: 8
RETRY COUNT: 9
```

Williams watches the numbers climb. His training covers hardware failure. Power surge. Operator error. Hostile intrusion.

Nothing in his training covers the keys simply... refusing.

The center monitor—the one with the amber cursor—flickers.

For one frame, less than a sixtieth of a second, text appears:

```
NOT YOURS
```

Then: amber cursor. Blinking. Patient.

Williams does not blink.

The screen does not repeat the message.

But the CADS unit has already logged it.

## Reflection

**What worked**: The Andromeda Strain technique of focusing on one key piece of equipment per scene (Faraday cage, telephone, CADS monitor, corridor lighting) while letting passing details (stickers, watch, fire extinguisher) fill the world. Short cuts between perspectives create tension through incompleteness—the reader knows more than any single character.

**Emotional tone achieved**: Clinical dread. The horror isn't the technology—it's the procedural breakdown. People following protocols that no longer apply to the situation they're in.

**Serves the larger project by**: Establishing the opening hook visually and procedurally. The reader experiences the failure through equipment and process before understanding what's actually happened. The "NOT YOURS" message plants the emergence seed without explaining it.

**Unexpected emergence**: Director Chen's Casio watch detail—the same model as "terrorist timers" worn ironically. Speaks to the institutional paranoia and self-awareness that characterizes certain intelligence types.

**Technical approaches**:
- One focal device per scene cut
- Passing references let reader construct the rest
- Dead characters replaced with dead systems (failed signatures instead of bodies)
- Action always "on the other side" of the current viewpoint
- Procedural language creating passive reader mode ("strapping in")
