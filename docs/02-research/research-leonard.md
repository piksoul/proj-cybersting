# Research - Elmore Leonard

## Overview

Elmore Leonard (1925-2013) was the **master of dialogue** in American crime fiction. His famous "10 Rules of Writing" distilled decades of craft into principles that prioritize **invisibility** — the author should never show. His novels read like transcriptions of real conversations between dangerous, self-deluded, and often very funny people.

> "If it sounds like writing, I rewrite it."

For The Satoshi Protocol, Leonard offers the essential lesson: **get out of the way and let characters reveal themselves**.

Leonard started in Westerns, then transitioned to crime fiction when the Western market declined. The transition revealed that his real subject was never genre — it was **character revealed through talk**.

## The 10 Rules of Writing

Leonard's famous rules from his 2001 essay:

| Rule | Description |
|------|-------------|
| 1 | Never open a book with weather |
| 2 | Avoid prologues |
| 3 | Never use a verb other than "said" to carry dialogue |
| 4 | Never use an adverb to modify "said" |
| 5 | Keep your exclamation points under control |
| 6 | Never use "suddenly" or "all hell broke loose" |
| 7 | Use regional dialect, patois, sparingly |
| 8 | Avoid detailed descriptions of characters |
| 9 | Don't go into great detail describing places and things |
| 10 | Leave out the part that readers tend to skip |

### The Meta-Rule

> "If it sounds like writing, I rewrite it."

The rules all serve one principle: **author invisibility**. Readers should experience the story, not notice the storyteller.

## The Leonard Style

### Dialogue as Everything

Leonard's novels are **70-80% dialogue**. Description is minimal; action is described briefly. The page is dominated by talk.

But it's not realistic dialogue — it's **heightened** dialogue that sounds real:

- Characters reveal themselves through speech patterns
- Self-delusion is audible
- Intelligence (or its absence) is demonstrated
- Menace conveyed through understatement

### Characters Who Don't Know They're Funny

Leonard's signature: **characters who take themselves seriously while being absurd**.

- Criminals with elaborate self-justifications
- Hitmen with pretensions
- Lowlifes with dignity
- Smart people making dumb choices

The comedy emerges from the gap between self-image and reality.

### Procedural Realism

Leonard researched obsessively:

- How criminals actually operate
- Police procedure
- Legal processes
- Specific industries (film, music, finance)

The details are accurate, but worn lightly — never showing off research.

### Point of View Fluidity

Leonard moves between POVs freely:

- Multiple limited third person
- Head-hopping within scenes (breaks conventional rules)
- Each character's voice distinct
- Reader assembles full picture from fragments

## Themes

### Crime as Work

Leonard's criminals treat crime as **a job**:

- Professional considerations (risk, reward, logistics)
- Workplace problems (unreliable partners, difficult bosses)
- Career progression (or lack thereof)
- The mundane reality of illegal work

### Self-Delusion

Everyone in Leonard's world thinks they're smarter than they are:

- Criminals who believe they've got it figured out
- Marks who don't know they're marks
- Heroes who barely stay ahead
- Villains undone by overconfidence

### Detroit/Florida Axis

Leonard's two primary settings:

**Detroit:**
- Working-class, industrial
- Urban decay, persistence
- Blue-collar criminals

**Florida:**
- Get-rich-quick, transient
- Sun-baked corruption
- White-collar crime in shorts

### Violence Without Glamour

Violence in Leonard is:

- Quick
- Often surprising
- Sometimes absurd
- Never glorified
- Has consequences

## Techniques for The Satoshi Protocol

### Dialogue-Driven Exposition

Leonard proves you can convey complex information through conversation without it feeling expository:

**Application:**

Instead of:
> The cryptographic protocol relied on elliptic curve mathematics...

Try:
> "So this thing can't be hacked?"
> "Nothing can't be hacked. But this — you'd need to break math."
> "Break math."
> "Elliptic curves. Multiply up easy, divide back down hard. That's the whole trick."
> "And someone broke the trick."
> "Or they were inside it from the start."

### Characters Who Think They're Smarter

Leonard's self-deluded characters map well to crypto world:

- True believers certain they've seen the future
- Financial types who think they understand technology
- Technologists who think they understand finance
- Everyone certain they're the smart money

### The Procedural Feel

Leonard's research-heavy approach works for technical thriller:

- Accurate crypto/financial detail
- Worn lightly, not showing off
- Characters who know their work
- Procedure as character revelation

### Invisible Author

Following Leonard's meta-rule:

- No passages that feel like "writing"
- Description only when necessary
- Let dialogue do the work
- Trust readers to assemble meaning

## What Leonard Does That We Should Borrow

| Technique | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Dialogue dominance** | 70%+ dialogue in key scenes |
| **"Said" only** | Invisible dialogue tags |
| **Self-deluded characters** | Crypto true believers, finance bros |
| **Procedural accuracy** | Technical detail worn lightly |
| **Author invisibility** | No "writing" showing |
| **Characters reveal themselves** | Speech patterns = character |
| **Comedy from gap** | Self-image vs. reality |

### What Leonard Does That We Should Adapt

| Leonard Approach | Our Adaptation |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Small-time criminals | Global-scale actors |
| Detroit/Florida settings | Global network geography |
| Blue-collar crime | White-collar/technical crime |
| Comic tone dominant | Comic moments in serious frame |
| Plot as excuse for characters | Plot matters alongside character |

### What Leonard Does That We Should Avoid

| Leonard Element | Why Avoid | Instead |
|-----------------|-----------|---------|
| **Head-hopping POV** | Can confuse | Clearer POV structure |
| **Primarily comic tone** | Need more tension | Comedy in service of thriller |
| **Minimal stakes** | Need weight | Existential stakes |
| **Meandering plots** | Need drive | Tighter thriller structure |
| **Contemporary realism** | We're near-future | Speculative elements |

## The Leonard Sound

Distinctive dialogue patterns:

### Trailing off
> "You think you can just—"
> "I think I can do what I want. That's what I think."

### The rhetorical question answered
> "Who's gonna stop me?"
> "Me."
> "You."
> "That's right."

### The understatement
> "This is going to be a problem."
> "You shot my brother."
> "That's what I mean."

## Integration Notes

Leonard offers:

1. **Dialogue technique** — conversation as primary mode
2. **Author invisibility** — get out of the way
3. **Comic timing** — self-delusion as comedy
4. **Procedural texture** — research worn lightly
5. **Character through speech** — people reveal themselves talking

His tone is lighter than our noir requires, but his **technique** is directly applicable.

**The synthesis:** Chandler's voice + Hammett's structure + Ellroy's intensity + Parker's pacing + Macdonald's depth + **Leonard's dialogue invisibility**.
