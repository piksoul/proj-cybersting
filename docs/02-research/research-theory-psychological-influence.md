# 1. Foundational Propaganda & Influence Theories

## Edward Bernays — Propaganda & Crystallizing Public Opinion  
Bernays viewed the public as irrational and highly influenceable. He pioneered modern PR, arguing that masses must be **guided by invisible hands** through carefully managed symbols, emotional appeals, and authority cues. His work explains how corporations and governments shape desires, consumer identity, and elections.

## Walter Lippmann — Public Opinion & Pseudo‑Environments  
Lippmann argued people don’t react to reality, but to **constructed mental images**—“pseudo‑environments.” Control the frame → control the behaviour. Highly relevant to narrative warfare and media manipulation.

## Jacques Ellul — Propaganda: The Formation of Men's Attitudes  
Ellul emphasised that propaganda must be **total**, continuous, and integrated into daily life. It creates conformity not through force but through **environmental saturation**.

## Noam Chomsky — Manufacturing Consent  
Chomsky’s model explains how democratic societies can be manipulated through **media filtering**, agenda-setting, and elite consensus management.

## Robert Cialdini — Influence (Six Principles)  
Authority, scarcity, reciprocity, liking, social proof, commitment/consistency — the mechanics of micro‑influence and persuasion.

# 2. Mass Psychology & Crowd Dynamics

## Gustave Le Bon — The Crowd  
Individuals in crowds become emotional, irrational, and suggestible. Identity dissolves and behaviour spreads contagiously. Highly relevant for riots, political rallies, revolutions.

## Mass Psychogenic Illness (MPI)  
Symptoms spread contagiously through **belief, expectation, and stress**, not through physical causes. Useful for portraying panic cascades, false alarms, or coordinated hysteria.

## Collective Delusion  
Shared beliefs emerge without external evidence. Examples: doomsday cults, moral panics, market bubbles.

## Contagion Theory  
Emotions, behaviours, and beliefs spread like viruses. Perfect for memetic warfare sequences.

## Social Identity & Deindividuation  
Group identity overrides personal identity, enabling extreme conformity, violence, or obedience.

# 3. Obedience, Authority & Compliance

## Milgram Experiment  
People obey authority even when actions conflict with morality. Core insight: **situational power overrides personal ethics**.

## Stanford Prison Experiment  
Role assignment and environment can produce abusive behaviour rapidly.

## Asch Conformity Experiments  
Individuals adopt group opinions even when obviously false.

## Bystander Effect  
Responsibility diffuses; groups reduce likelihood of action.

## Cognitive Dissonance (Festinger)  
People rationalize behaviours that contradict their beliefs, especially after committing publicly.

# 4. Behavioural Economics & Modern Influence

## Nudge Theory  
Behaviour can be shaped subtly by altering choice architecture — defaults, framing, and friction.

## Behavioural Economics  
People are predictably irrational; biases can be systematically exploited.

## Predictive Algorithms  
AI uses behaviour patterns to anticipate and manipulate future actions.

## Attention Engineering  
Platforms weaponize dopamine cycles to capture and redirect cognition.

## Hyperreality (Baudrillard)  
Media replaces reality with simulation; perception becomes more important than truth.

## Media Ecology (McLuhan)  
“The medium is the message” — technology reshapes society more than content.

# 5. Psychological Warfare Techniques

## Gaslighting  
Destabilising perception of reality.

## Narrative Priming  
Setting context so that future messages are accepted more easily.

## Trauma Bonding  
Using cycles of fear and reward to create loyalty.

## Fear Conditioning  
Associating concepts with fear triggers compliance.

## Identity Fragmentation  
Manipulating or eroding identity to rebuild loyalty or dependence.
