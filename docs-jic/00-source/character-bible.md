## PROTAGONIST

### Elias Reed — LOCKED (core) / UNLOCKED (backstory detail)

**Role:** Former NSA cryptographer turned academic. Protagonist.

**Age:** Early 30s.

**Background:** Worked in NSA's crypto division before leaving. Now at a university running cryptographic research. The reasons he left NSA are personal and unresolved — he saw how secrets get owned, buried, weaponised. Academic life was supposed to be clean.

**Appearance:** TBD — not yet specified in planning docs.

**Voice:** Clipped, precise. Dry wit under pressure. Thinks in systems and patterns before he thinks in emotions. Short sentences. Doesn't explain himself unless forced.

**Psychology:** Guarded. Compartmentalises. Sees the world as interlocking systems — monetary, cryptographic, social. This makes him brilliant at pattern recognition and terrible at trusting people. He left the NSA to escape secrets that had owners, but can't stop finding them.

**Flaw:** His analytical detachment is a defence mechanism. He observes when he should act. He systemises when he should feel. This makes him slow to trust, slow to commit, and vulnerable to being outmanoeuvred by people who act on instinct.

**Arc direction:** Must move from observer to participant. The story forces him to stop analysing and choose — not the optimal outcome, but a human one. By the climax, his decision is emotional, not calculated.

**Personal connection to sabotage:** TBD — the idea-recap flags this as needed. One possibility seeded in the test import: he recognises the failure pattern because he designed a version of it in a prior context. This creates guilt, complicity, and personal stakes.

**Key relationships:**
- The Mentor (see below) — knew fragments of the truth
- Nyx — old darknet contact, points him toward the ghost in the code
- The Saboteur — philosophical mirror / antagonist

**What an agent needs for scene generation:**
- POV is tight third person, present tense, from Elias's perspective (unless noted otherwise)
- Internal monologue is analytical, not lyrical — systems metaphors, pattern language
- Under stress he gets quieter, not louder
- Dry humour surfaces in the worst moments

## ACT I SUPPORTING CAST

### Secretary of the Treasury — UNLOCKED

**Role:** Public face of the key activation ceremony. Political appointee, not a technologist.

**Function in story:** Represents institutional overconfidence. Present in the SCIF when the keys fail (Ch3-4). Reacts with political panic, not technical understanding.

**Voice direction:** Formal, controlled, breaks under pressure. The kind of person who asks "who authorised this?" when they should be asking "what happened?"

**Profile needed:** Name, political background, relationship to the shadow directorate. Currently a role, not a character.

### NSA Crypto Division Lead — UNLOCKED

**Role:** The quiet, dangerous one in the room. Knows more than they reveal.

**Function in story:** Potential handler, potential antagonist. Present at the key ceremony. Glances at Elias when the sabotage is discovered — suggesting foreknowledge or suspicion.

**Voice direction:** Minimal speech. When they talk, it matters. Cold precision. Military-adjacent diction.

**Profile needed:** Name, rank/title, relationship to Elias's NSA past, whether they're aligned with the shadow directorate or running their own agenda.

### Deputy Undersecretary for Strategic Value Systems — UNLOCKED

**Role:** Shadow operator within Treasury. The person who actually understands the monetary weapon being deployed.

**Function in story:** Bridges the political and operational layers. Knows about the BTC standard plan. May or may not know about the sabotage.

**Voice direction:** Bureaucratic precision masking ruthlessness. Speaks in policy language that conceals intent.

**Profile needed:** Full character development. Currently a title, not a person.

## ACT II+ CAST (sketched, not developed)

### Nyx — UNLOCKED

**Role:** Old contact from Elias's past. Darknet-adjacent. Points him toward the ghost in the code (Ch7).

**Function in story:** Information broker and trust test. Represents the world Elias left behind.

**Profile needed:** Everything — background, appearance, voice, motivations, relationship history with Elias.

### The Mentor — UNLOCKED

**Role:** Elias's former mentor, involved in early Bitcoin proto-code (Ch15 reveal).

**Function in story:** The emotional betrayal and the bridge between Elias's past and the sabotage's origin. Knew fragments of the truth.

**Profile needed:** Full development. Critical for Act II emotional stakes. The mentor's identity and relationship to the Saboteur is a load-bearing narrative decision.

### The Saboteur — UNLOCKED (identity is a climax decision)

**Role:** The entity or person who swapped the Satoshi key signatures.

**Competing concepts (from planning docs):**
1. Former NSA cryptographer (human, rogue insider)
2. Satoshi themselves (if alive)
3. Secretive open-source anarchist collective
4. Emergent behaviour from early mining code (not literal AI — distributed organism)
5. Splinter faction inside the U.S. government
6. Elias's mentor

**Narrative function:** Philosophical mirror to Elias. The saboteur's ideology drives the thematic core — "sovereignty is a story governments tell themselves."

**What's decided:** The saboteur is not a cartoon villain. Their logic is coherent and arguably correct. They believe the keys should not be controlled by any nation-state.

**What's not decided:** Whether they're human, collective, or emergent. Whether they're alive or a dead researcher's contingency. Whether Elias ultimately agrees with them.
